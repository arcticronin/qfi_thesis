\chapter{Conclusions and Outlook}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This thesis examined quantum Fisher information from two complementary perspectives: its scalable estimation on quantum hardware and its operational role in sub-Rayleigh optical sensing. The common goal is to retain metrological information when a direct calculation or a direct measurement becomes impractical.

The first part established the QFI formalism and presented variational, dynamics-agnostic bounds based on VQFIE and VQSE. The open-system study then showed how the metrological response of a reduced sensor depends on the structure of the noise channel, not solely on its purity. Together, these chapters frame QFI as both a theoretical limit and a quantity requiring experimentally feasible estimators.

The astrophysical part formulated high-contrast exoplanet detection as mixed-state estimation and studied SPADE as a receiver that directs the dominant star signal and the displacement-sensitive signal into separate spatial-mode sectors. The accompanying calibration and count-channel analysis connect the idealized receiver description to the experimentally observed crosstalk, attenuation, and overdispersion.

Finally, the 2-basis projection framework made explicit the limited but useful correspondence between the variational and optical pictures. Spectral rank truncation and a low-order HG modal cutoff are distinct constructions; under a weak-source, high-contrast, aligned Gaussian model they nevertheless isolate the same dominant physical component and allow their QFI bounds to be compared. This distinction defines a clear route for future work: extend the comparison beyond the small-separation model, quantify its robustness under imperfect alignment and richer spatial modes, and test VQSE-assisted metrology directly on quantum hardware.
