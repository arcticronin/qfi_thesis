\chapter{Conclusions and Outlook}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This thesis examined quantum Fisher information from two complementary perspectives: its scalable estimation on quantum hardware and its role as a benchmark for sub-Rayleigh optical sensing. The common goal is to retain and assess metrological information when a direct calculation or a direct measurement becomes impractical.

The first part established the QFI formalism and presented variational, dynamics-agnostic bounds based on VQFIE and VQSE. The open-system study then showed how the metrological response of a reduced sensor depends on the structure of the noise channel, not solely on its purity. Together, these chapters frame QFI as both a theoretical limit and a quantity requiring experimentally feasible estimators.

The astrophysical part formulated high-contrast exoplanet detection as mixed-state estimation and introduced SPADE as a receiver that directs the dominant star signal and the displacement-sensitive signal into separate spatial-mode sectors. The practical analysis then showed that the available aggregate first-order count record cannot reconstruct a low-mode quantum state or directly yield TQFI. It instead supports a channel-resolved, conditional analysis: a specified loss, alignment, readout, background, and overdispersion model can be fitted and tested against repeated counts, after which its QFI and measurement CFI provide clearly labelled design benchmarks.

The main practical outlook is therefore calibration rather than overinterpretation. Recording the individual HG outputs, a complement/no-click outcome, accepted trials, source-blocked backgrounds, and a mode-injection confusion matrix would turn the present conditional model into a calibrated multi-outcome SPADE experiment. It would then be possible to estimate the CFI of the actual receiver, compare it robustly with a validated model QFI, and decide whether further spatial modes or coherence-sensitive measurements are worth the additional experimental complexity.
