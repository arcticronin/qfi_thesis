\chapter{SPADE as a Quantum-to-Classical Channel}
\label{ch:exoplanet_experiment}

Chapter~\ref{sec:astrophysical_metrology_spade} introduced SPADE.  This project began with the aim of applying the truncated-QFI methods of Chapter~\ref{ch:methods} to experimental SPADE data provided by \textbf{ASI Matera}~\cite{Santamaria2024single}.  The experiment had already ended when the dataset became available for this thesis.  It was therefore not possible to add the extra measurements needed for state reconstruction.  The recorded data contain one aggregate first-order count for each repetition, but not the separate modal outputs and coherence-sensitive measurements needed to build a reduced optical state.  This record cannot support experimental state reconstruction or an experimental TQFI calculation.

This limitation led to a change in perspective.  First, a classical statistical model was fitted to determine which features of the measured counts could be predicted reliably.  The experiment was then described as a sequential quantum-to-classical channel model.  This model separates the parameterized source state, quantum propagation, accessible-mode reduction, measurement, classical readout, calibration, and count process.  It also states which parameters are fitted, fixed by external calibration, assumed, or not identifiable.  The data constrain only the population-level projection of this sequence, while the assumed quantum model provides checkpoints for conditional QFI and CFI calculations.

The resulting framework is foundation work for future SPADE experiments.  It shows how a practical channel model can be fitted without claiming that the available counts reconstruct the full quantum channel.  It also provides a clear basis for possible future collaboration with ASI Matera.  A new experiment could be planned from the start to record the separate modal, flux, failure, and coherence-sensitive measurements required for an experimental link between SPADE and TQFI.

\begin{remark}[Central conclusion of this chapter]
    The quantum-channel framework provides a physically complete and modular description of the experiment and enables conditional QFI calculations. The current aggregate-count data constrain only its population-level classical projection~\footnote{the classical probabilities of the model, or diagonal elements of its density matrix} . They do not reconstruct the complete quantum channel or its coherence dynamics
\end{remark}



\section{SPADE integration into TQFI formalism}
\label{sec:spade_question_record}

\subsection{The low-mode link to TQFI}

The connection to TQFI comes from the low-order SPADE receiver in Chapter~\ref{sec:astrophysical_metrology_spade} and the rank-truncation methods in Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}.  A displaced point-spread function has support in an infinite-dimensional HG space, although its lowest orders carry most of the displacement response near alignment.  Relating these measured modes to TQFI requires three different reductions to be kept separate.

\paragraph{Numerical HG cutoff.}
A numerical cutoff approximates the infinite-dimensional source model by keeping HG modes up to a stated order.  Its accuracy is checked by increasing the cutoff until the calculated quantities converge.  It is a computational approximation, not a restriction imposed by the apparatus.

\paragraph{Accessible-mode channel.}
The apparatus gives access to a fixed set of modes.  A physical reduction to this set must keep the probability outside it as a complement or failure outcome.  For a selected subspace with projector $P_M$, we use the following flagged channel.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_M(\rho)
    =P_M\rho P_M
    +\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(\mathbb{I}-P_M)\rho\right]
    |\mathrm{fail}\rangle\langle\mathrm{fail}|
    \label{eq:spade_accessible_projection}
\end{equation}
The failure flag keeps the probability in unobserved modes.  Discarding this probability and renormalizing $P_M\rho P_M$ would introduce postselection and could overstate the available information.

\paragraph{Rank-$m$ TQFI.}
Rank-$m$ TQFI instead keeps the $m$ dominant eigenvectors of the parameterized density operator.  These state-dependent eigenvectors do not have to match the HG modes selected by the apparatus.  This reduction therefore requires a reconstructed parameterized state rather than a chosen set of detector ports.


\subsection{The available count \enquote{cube}}

The recorded observable is defined below.
\begin{equation}
    n_1=n_{HG_{01}}+n_{HG_{10}}
    \qquad
    \texttt{H\_data}[d_a,\epsilon,r]=n_{1,r}
    \label{eq:spade_recorded_count}
\end{equation}
Here, $d_a=d/w_0$ is the signed nominal displacement, $\epsilon$ is the faint-source fraction defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:intensity_ratio_epsilon}, and $r$ indexes repetitions.  The cube contains $21\times25\times100=52{,}500$ integer counts, with $d_a\in[-0.66667,0.66667]$, $\epsilon\in[0.00427,0.09593]$, and $100$ independent $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ repetitions at every setting, see Figure~\ref{fig:spade_count_data}.

The missing outcomes set the limit of the analysis.  Separate $HG_{01}$ and $HG_{10}$ counts would resolve two orthogonal first-order directions, while $HG_{00}$ together with aggregate $HG_1$ would provide a reference and a separation-sensitive outcome.  The record contains neither pair.  A nonzero $n_1$ at $d_a=0$ can also combine background, bright-mode leakage, coupling variation, alignment error, and higher-order leakage.  Dividing $n_1$ by an assumed photon number and setting $p_0=1-p_1$ would hide these contributions in the remainder.  Since the data contain no phase-sensitive observable, they also cannot determine off-diagonal HG coherences.  The record can test forward count models, but experimental TQFI requires additional modal and coherence-sensitive measurements.

\section{The Sequential Quantum-to-Classical Fitting Method}
\label{sec:spade_sequential_channels}
% ==============================================================================

We use the density-operator, quantum-channel, and POVM conventions introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}, with supplementary generalized-measurement details collected in Appendix~\ref{app:mathematical_background}. Here we specify only the physical action and experiment-specific parameters of each channel.

The complete ordering is
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small]
    source preparation
    -> survival / erasure
    -> displacement and sorter alignment
    -> possible HG-basis dephasing
    -> possible coherent HG-mode mixing
    -> mode-dependent loss into a failure sector
    -> flagged accessible-mode reduction
    -> imperfect / limited-outcome SPADE measurement
    -> classical readout / cross-talk
    -> background and calibration variation
    -> discrete repeated-count observation.
\end{Verbatim}
This separation has physical meaning.  Loss, displacement, dephasing, and coherent modal mixing act before measurement and may change the quantum state.  The SPADE POVM converts that state into classical outcome probabilities.  Electronic or reported-port cross-talk, additive background, calibration variation, and count noise act on classical outcomes.  They should be placed before measurement only if an independent physical model supports that choice.

\subsection{Classical and quantum objects in the model}

A classical state is a probability vector $\boldsymbol p$.  In a chosen basis, it may be embedded as a diagonal density matrix.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{diag}}=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol p)
    \label{eq:spade_classical_embedding}
\end{equation}
A general quantum state can also contain off-diagonal coherences.  A classical stochastic channel maps probability vectors to probability vectors.  A quantum channel acts on the complete density operator and can describe both populations and coherences.  Measurement produces a classical vector $p_y=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho M_y)$.  Detector reporting and repeated-count statistics then act on that vector.  A probability vector is not a quantum channel or a substitute for the pre-measurement state.

The main advantage of the quantum description is not the number of adjustable parameters.  It is the ability to represent physically meaningful coherence, interference, loss, mode mixing, and open-system dynamics while preserving positivity and normalization.  Table~\ref{tab:spade_channel_sequence} records the classification and provenance needed to keep those roles distinct.

\subsection{Source preparation and the HG convention}

The spatial one-photon sector is not limited to two dimensions.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{spatial}}
    =\operatorname{span}\{|HG_{00}\rangle,|HG_{01}\rangle,|HG_{10}\rangle,|HG_{02}\rangle,\ldots\}
    \label{eq:spade_infinite_hg_space}
\end{equation}
For a one-photon event before apparatus loss, we use the following mutually incoherent bright/faint source state.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_S(d_a,\epsilon)
    =(1-\epsilon)|HG_{00}\rangle\langle HG_{00}|
    +\epsilon|\psi_{d_a}\rangle\langle\psi_{d_a}|
    \label{eq:spade_source_state}
\end{equation}
The sorter origin is aligned to the bright source, as in the experiment~\cite{Santamaria2024single}.  For the scan direction, the displaced state has the following expansion.
\begin{equation}
    |\psi_{d_a}\rangle
    =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-q/2}\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|HG_n\rangle
    \qquad
    \alpha=\frac{d_a}{2}
    \qquad
    q=|\alpha|^2=\frac{d_a^2}{4}
    \label{eq:spade_effective_coordinates}
\end{equation}
The modal probabilities follow from this expansion.
\begin{equation}
    P(HG_n|\psi_{d_a})=e^{-q}\frac{q^n}{n!}
    \qquad
    p_1(d_a,\epsilon)=\epsilon q e^{-q}
    \label{eq:spade_sector_probabilities}
\end{equation}
Here $p_1$ is the ideal aggregate first-order probability for the one-axis displacement model used by the count analysis.

Although $|\psi_{d_a}\rangle$ has nonzero amplitudes in infinitely many HG modes, the one-photon state in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spade_source_state} is a mixture of at most two pure states.  Its rank is at most two.  Infinite basis support and density-matrix rank are different concepts.  Numerical calculations use HG orders through $12$ and an unresolved tail flag.  Increasing the cutoff from order $8$ to $12$ changes the reported QFI by at most $5.1\times10^{-12}$ relative.

\subsection{Pre-measurement quantum channels}

\paragraph{Survival or erasure.}
For a one-photon input, the following enlarged-space model represents parameter-independent survival.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{surv}}
    =\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\rho_S)
    =\eta\rho_S+(1-\eta)|\mathrm{vac}\rangle\langle\mathrm{vac}|
    \label{eq:spade_loss_channel}
\end{equation}
This quantum erasure channel represents propagation, collection, and detection survival when these processes occur before the modelled measurement.  The current file does not measure its parameter $\eta$ because it does not record the number $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ of incident photons per acquisition.

\paragraph{Alignment and displacement.}
A fixed displacement of the sorter basis is a parameter-independent unitary channel.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{align}}
    =U_{\mathrm{align}}(\delta_{\mathrm{sorter}})
    \rho_{\mathrm{surv}}
    U_{\mathrm{align}}^{\dagger}(\delta_{\mathrm{sorter}})
    \label{eq:spade_alignment_channel}
\end{equation}
It preserves state QFI, but it can reduce the CFI of a fixed downstream SPADE basis.  The fitted $\delta$ in the empirical mean model is an effective displacement and calibration offset.  It is not an independent measurement of $\delta_{\mathrm{sorter}}$.  Random displacement or pointing jitter is instead a mixture over displacement unitaries and can reduce coherence.  It appears only as a provisional workbench channel below.

\paragraph{HG-basis dephasing.}
The following channel represents possible phase scrambling between HG amplitudes.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{v}(\rho)
    =v\rho+(1-v)\operatorname{diag}_{HG}(\rho)
    \qquad 0\leq v\leq1
    \label{eq:spade_quantum_sequence}
\end{equation}
This quantum channel suppresses off-diagonal HG coherences while keeping every modal population unchanged.  It may change state QFI, but the current diagonal aggregate measurement is exactly insensitive to $v$.  Its strength cannot be identified from these data.

\paragraph{Coherent mode mixing.}
The following operator represents coherent coupling of $HG_0$ and $HG_1$ on that subspace.
\begin{equation}
    U_{01}(\vartheta,\phi)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \cos\vartheta          & -e^{-i\phi}\sin\vartheta \\
        e^{i\phi}\sin\vartheta & \cos\vartheta
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:spade_coherent_mixing}
\end{equation}
For fixed $\vartheta$ and $\phi$, this is a unitary quantum channel.  It preserves state QFI while changing the part that a fixed SPADE measurement accesses.  No complex mode-transfer measurement is available here.  Its workbench values are invented assumptions for sensitivity analysis.

\paragraph{Mode-dependent loss.}
If mode $j$ has transmission $\tau_j$, the operator $L=\sum_j\sqrt{\tau_j}|HG_j\rangle\langle HG_j|$ attenuates the surviving block.  The following map places the missing probability in an orthogonal flag.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol\tau}(\rho)
    =L\rho L^{\dagger}
    +\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(\mathbb{I}-L^{\dagger}L)\rho\right]
    |\mathrm{fail}\rangle\langle\mathrm{fail}|
    \label{eq:spade_modal_loss}
\end{equation}
This construction preserves probability in the enlarged space.  Mode-dependent transmission can affect accessible-state QFI and measurement CFI.  The current record contains no absolute transmission for each mode, so $\tau_j$ is provisional in the workbench.  The accessible-mode channel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spade_accessible_projection} is applied after these pre-measurement maps.  It also keeps its complement instead of renormalizing it away.

\subsection{Measurement and post-measurement classical layers}

\paragraph{SPADE measurement.}
Using the POVM convention of Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}, we specify the ideal low-order outcomes below.
\begin{align}
    M_0               & =|HG_{00}\rangle\langle HG_{00}|\nonumber \\
    M_1               & =|HG_{01}\rangle\langle HG_{01}|
    +|HG_{10}\rangle\langle HG_{10}|\nonumber                     \\
    M_{\mathrm{fail}} & =\mathbb{I}-M_0-M_1
    \label{eq:spade_imperfect_povm}
\end{align}
The accessible state gives probabilities $p_y=\operatorname{Tr}(M_y\rho_M)$.  A complete low-order experiment would record all three outcomes and ideally record the two first-order ports separately.  In the practical model, the pre-measurement quantum channels contain optical mixing and collection loss.  Coarse-graining of the POVM represents limited outcome resolution.  The available file stores only repeated counts assigned to the coarse-grained $M_1$ result.

\paragraph{Classical readout and cross-talk.}
Once the POVM has produced the probability vector $\boldsymbol p$, with components $p_0$, $p_1$, and $p_{\mathrm{fail}}$, reported-port confusion is a classical stochastic channel $\boldsymbol p_{\mathrm{rep}}=R\boldsymbol p$.  The identifiable sequential fit uses the readout matrix below.
\begin{equation}
    R_{\chi}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1-\chi & \chi   & 0 \\
        \chi   & 1-\chi & 0 \\
        0      & 0      & 1
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad \chi=0.0035
    \label{eq:spade_readout_matrix}
\end{equation}
The value $\chi=0.0035$ is fixed externally from the paper's reported cross-talk calibration~\cite{Santamaria2024single}.  The paper does not give a complete measured transfer matrix.  The reverse element, equality of the two off-diagonal elements, and isolation of the failure outcome are clear assumptions.  This classical map can reduce measurement CFI, but it does not act on the pre-measurement density operator.

\paragraph{Calibration, background, and counts.}
In the final stages, an additive background $B$, a multiplicative coupling field $g(d_a,\epsilon)$, and a discrete repeated-count law act on the reported probability.  We use the following sequential mean.
\begin{equation}
    \mu(d_a,\epsilon)
    =B+T\,g(d_a,\epsilon)\,p_{\mathrm{rep},1}(d_a,\epsilon)
    \qquad
    T=N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta
    \label{eq:spade_count_channel}
\end{equation}
Here $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ denotes the incident photons in one $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ acquisition.  The parameter $\eta$ denotes their survival probability under the stated pre-measurement convention.  The product $T$ is the identifiable conditional throughput scale.  A repeated count is then written as $n_{1,r}\sim P_{\mathrm{count}}(\mu,\boldsymbol\zeta)$, where $\boldsymbol\zeta$ contains classical dispersion or tail parameters.  Background, calibration variation, and the count law may change final count statistics and count CFI.  They do not change the state QFI upstream of the POVM.

The mean also shows the main quantities that cannot be separated.  The signal constrains products such as $N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta$ and modal transmission more directly than their separate factors.  The baseline can trade optical leakage against additive background.  A good fit to $\mu$ cannot uniquely validate all pre-measurement quantum maps.

\subsection{Why the channel formulation is useful}

The sequential framework has eight practical advantages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Physical validity.} Quantum states and pre-measurement maps preserve positivity and total probability.  Loss is sent to vacuum or a flag instead of being silently discarded.
    \item \emph{Treatment of coherence.} Modal populations are separated from off-diagonal coherences, so dephasing and coherent interference can be represented even though the current counts cannot infer them.
    \item \emph{Separation of mechanisms.} Photon loss, alignment, dephasing, coherent mixing, modal transmission, readout cross-talk, background, calibration variation, and count noise occupy distinct layers.
    \item \emph{Correct information accounting.} Source-state QFI, post-channel QFI, accessible-state QFI, ideal-SPADE CFI, imperfect-readout CFI, and final count CFI can be compared at named checkpoints, with per-photon and per-acquisition normalizations kept separate.
    \item \emph{Modularity.} An improved calibration can replace one map or matrix without redesigning the source, inference, or QFI pipeline.
    \item \emph{Identifiability.} The product form makes clear why the data identify $T=N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta$ more easily than $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\eta$ separately.
    \item \emph{Experimental design.} Each map that cannot be identified points directly to an additional measurement, such as a flux monitor, mode-injection matrix, failure count, or coherence-sensitive receiver.
    \item \emph{Provenance.} Every parameter can be labelled as measured, externally calibrated, fitted, assumed, or invented for sensitivity analysis.
\end{enumerate}

For parameter-independent maps, the matching information checkpoints follow the data-processing order shown below.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_Q(\rho_S)
    \geq \mathcal{F}_Q(\rho_{\mathrm{post}})
    \geq \mathcal{F}_Q[\mathcal{E}_M(\rho_{\mathrm{post}})]
    \geq \mathcal{F}_{C}(\text{SPADE})
    \geq \mathcal{F}_{C}(\text{readout})
    \label{eq:spade_information_processing_chain}
\end{equation}
Equality holds across a fixed unitary at the state-QFI checkpoint.  Per-surviving-photon quantities become per-acquisition resources only after multiplication by a justified photon number or throughput.  The behavior of a stochastic calibration field during an unknown-source measurement must also be specified before a final count CFI can be defined.

\subsection{Limitations of the formulation}

Adding channel parameters does not by itself make a model more scientifically valid.  Coherence parameters cannot be inferred from diagonal aggregate counts, and several quantum channels can have the same population-level projection.  A good fit of final count means does not uniquely validate the full channel sequence.  Invented or weakly constrained values must not be presented as experimental measurements.  In particular, the current record provides neither quantum process tomography nor a directly reconstructed experimental QFI.  These limits explain the clear provenance labels used in Sections~\ref{sec:spade_conditional_inference} and~\ref{sec:spade_qfi_role}.

Exposure accounting is essential.  Every stored value is a $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ count, while the paper combines the $100$ repetitions and quotes about $40{,}000$--$80{,}000$ detected photons for one second.  The comparable first-order observable is the one-second sum below.
\begin{equation}
    n_{1,\mathrm{1\,s}}=\sum_{r=1}^{100}n_{1,r}
    \label{eq:spade_one_second_count}
\end{equation}
This is not the average $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ count.  The median zero-separation aggregate is $817$ counts per second.  We use the following paper-style regression.
\begin{equation}
    n_{1,\mathrm{1\,s}}
    =\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}(\eta n)_{\mathrm{1\,s}}
    +c\epsilon d_a^2
    \label{eq:spade_paper_calibration}
\end{equation}
For the assumed normalization $(\eta n)_{\mathrm{1\,s}}=40{,}000$, the fitted values are shown below.
\begin{equation}
    \chi_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.020743\pm0.000109
    \qquad
    c=27{,}282.7\pm430.1
    \qquad R^2=0.88499
    \label{eq:spade_paper_calibration_values}
\end{equation}
For an $80{,}000$ normalization, $c$ is unchanged and $\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.010371\pm0.000054$.  These are \emph{effective baseline fractions}, not measurements of pure $HG_{00}$-to-$HG_1$ cross-talk.  The paper value $\chi=0.0035$ would contribute only $140$ or $280$ baseline counts at the two one-second normalizations.  These values are well below the observed offset of about $830$ counts.  Background, drift, mismatch, and normalization differences may all contribute.

The incident-photon, detected-photon, and exposure quantities must remain distinct.  The quantity $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the unrecorded number incident in one $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ repetition.  The product $\eta N_{\mathrm{in}}=T$ is a conditional detected-throughput scale for that repetition.  The value $n_{1,r}$ is the much smaller reported-port count.  Equation~\eqref{eq:spade_one_second_count} sums $100$ such reported counts.  The paper's $40{,}000$--$80{,}000$ is a one-second detected-photon normalization, not $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ or a per-repeat count.

\subsection{Discrete count diagnostics}

The setting means are only about $7$--$19$ counts, so a Gaussian quasi-likelihood is not used as the main count law.  Gaussian parametric bootstraps can generate non-integer and even negative observations.  The discrete diagnostic models instead keep the counts as non-negative integers.

The following first two moments define the NB1 family.
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{E}(Y)=\mu
    \qquad
    \operatorname{Var}(Y)=F\mu
    \label{eq:spade_nb1_moments}
\end{equation}
It reduces to Poisson at $F=1$.  With saturated setting means, it gives $F=2.4188$ and a negative log likelihood of $153{,}969.7$.  A Poisson--lognormal model gives a log-rate standard deviation of $0.3765$ and a negative log likelihood of $153{,}893.2$.  This improves AIC by $153.0$.  The latter model reproduces the observed number of zero counts, but it still fails checks of variance differences and median skewness within settings.  The two-sided bootstrap $p$-values are $0.00664$ and $0.0133$, respectively.

Neither one-parameter family reproduces all the individual-count variance and tail structure.  The hierarchical analysis uses the empirical standard errors of the $100$ repetitions to predict setting means.  At the recorded settings, the observed repetitions are resampled to estimate uncertainty instead of treating either parametric family as exact.

\subsection{A fixed benchmark channel is rejected}

The fixed scenario uses one example $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ normalization, $N_{\mathrm{in}}=40{,}000$.  It also uses $\chi=0.0035$, $HG_1$ transmission $0.9965$, zero higher-order leakage and background, zero offset, unit PSF scale and source-ratio exponent, and fixed $F=2.6$.  The value $40{,}000$ per $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ is a scenario assumption.  It is not the paper's one-second detected-photon number.

The optimization within this fixed conditional model gives the values below.
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \widehat{\eta}=0.0509391
        \qquad &
        \eta\in[0.0505743,0.0513051]
        \\
               & \text{at conditional }99.9\%\text{ profile confidence}
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:spade_conditional_eta}
\end{equation}
However, the observed setting-mean Pearson discrepancy is $4327.62$, compared with a bootstrap median of $524.73$.  The following $p$-value comes from $200$ discrete, refitted simulations.
\begin{equation}
    p=\frac{1}{201}\simeq0.00498
    \label{eq:spade_fixed_rejection}
\end{equation}
The model is rejected.  The narrow interval in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spade_conditional_eta} shows only a narrow optimum within this conditional scenario.  The quantity $\widehat\eta$ is not a measured physical efficiency or the main experimental result.

\subsection{Hierarchical calibration model}

The main positive result uses the physical source response as its central mean.
\begin{equation}
    \mu_0(d_a,\epsilon)
    =B+A\epsilon q e^{-q}
    \qquad
    q=\frac{(d_a-\delta)^2}{4}
    \label{eq:spade_effective_count_model}
\end{equation}
Here, $\delta$ is a dimensionless offset in units of $w_0$.  The model uses the bright-source-aligned convention $q=d_{\mathrm{eff}}^2/4$ with $d_{\mathrm{eff}}=d_a-\delta$.
The following relation describes the remaining response variation.
\begin{equation}
    \log\!\frac{\mu(d_a,\epsilon)}{\mu_0(d_a,\epsilon)}
    \sim \text{Mat\'ern-$3/2$ Gaussian process}
    +\text{independent setting scatter}
    \label{eq:spade_calibration_gp}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian process is a \emph{classical calibration channel} placed after the ideal optical response.  It represents coupling or calibration variation.  It is not a new quantum effect or source-encoded separation information.

The all-grid fit gives $B=8.23045$ counts per $10\,\mathrm{ms}$, $A=1225.58$ counts, and $\delta=0.009824$.  This corresponds to a physical offset of $0.009824w_0$.  The fitted correlated and independent log-response standard deviations are $0.05248$ and $0.02520$, which correspond to about $5.25\%$ and $2.52\%$.  The correlation lengths are $0.0597w_0$ in displacement and $0.0228$ in $\epsilon$.  The effective ratio $B/A=0.006716$ has the same algebraic form as a leakage fraction.  However, it is not an optical cross-talk probability unless the background is negligible and the first-order normalization is known independently.

\subsection{The identifiable sequential-channel scale}

The sequential fit combines the source, the flagged $HG_0/HG_1$ reduction, the externally fixed readout in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spade_readout_matrix}, a fitted background, a zero-mean coupling field, and an empirical repeated-count layer.  The zero weighted mean convention prevents the coupling field from absorbing the global scale.  Since the file contains neither launched photons nor an absolute $HG_1$ coupling, the main quantity that can be estimated is the conditional throughput.
\begin{equation}
    T=N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta
    \label{eq:spade_effective_parameter_combinations}
\end{equation}
This quantity is given per $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ and is conditional on the readout and calibration conventions.

\section{Conditional Information After the Channel Model}
\label{sec:spade_qfi_role}
% ==============================================================================

The definitions and numerical methods are those of Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}.  No information quantity is calculated by substituting empirical count variance into a QFI formula.  For the specified channel parameters $\boldsymbol\lambda$, the model quantity used here is defined below.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_{Q}^{\mathrm{model}}(d_a)
    =\mathcal{F}_{Q}\!\left[
        \rho_{\mathrm{post}}(d_a,\epsilon;\boldsymbol\lambda)
        \right]
    \label{eq:spade_model_qfi}
\end{equation}
It is evaluated from the assumed parameterized pre-measurement state for estimation of $d_a$.  Measurement CFI is then evaluated after the stated SPADE and readout maps.  These calculations show where a proposed instrument can lose information.  They do not reconstruct experimental QFI from $n_1$.

\subsection{Numerical validation and compatible comparisons}

The ideal source calculation uses the bright-source-aligned $\alpha=d_a/2$ convention, an HG-order-$12$ basis, and a flagged numerical tail.  At regular nonzero settings, the maximum relative difference between the SLD and independent finite-fidelity QFI calculations is $0.322\%$.  The order-$8$ to order-$12$ cutoff change is at most $5.1\times10^{-12}$ relative.  The point $d_a=0$ is a rank boundary and is excluded from the quoted regular-point validation ranges, consistent with Appendix~\ref{app:mathematical_background}.

\section{Supported Conclusions and Measurement Priorities}
\label{sec:spade_conclusions}
% ==============================================================================

\begin{remark}[Supported conclusions]
    The count cube contains a separation-dependent first-order signal, a nonzero baseline, and uneven super-Poissonian variation across repetitions.  The fixed benchmark channel and the three deterministic extensions are rejected.  A physics-informed hierarchical classical calibration channel predicts held-out $100$-repeat setting means.  The standardized-residual SD is about $1.00$--$1.04$, and nominal $95\%$ coverage is about $93.5\%$--$94.7\%$.  Under the stated readout and zero-mean coupling conventions, the identifiable scale is the conditional throughput $T=1304.25$ per $10\,\mathrm{ms}$, with a grouped cross-fit envelope of $[1251.49,1359.47]$.  For the assumed source model, full ideal SPADE is nearly QFI-optimal.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Claims not supported by the record]
    The data do not reconstruct the complete quantum channel, its coherence dynamics, or a reduced density matrix.  They do not identify $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\eta$ separately, resolve background from cross-talk, validate a complete parametric distribution for every individual count, perform quantum process tomography, or yield an experimental QFI.  A fitted mean does not uniquely validate all upstream maps, and provisional workbench parameters are not experimental measurements.
\end{remark}

The most valuable future measurements are those that separate the layers that the current data cannot distinguish.

Independent coherence-sensitive data or a separately validated channel would then support comparison with state QFI or TQFI.  This ordering avoids treating population counts as quantum tomography.

\subsection{Reproducibility and final perspective}

The analysis code and machine-readable results are kept under \texttt{cqes/exoplanets} in the external QCES analysis repository.  The thesis uses copies of the generated figures under \texttt{TeXtured/figures/exoplanets}.  A reproducible run records the source convention, exposure duration, count cube, discrete diagnostic model, bootstrap or resampling seed, channel order, and the source of every parameter.  Machine-specific or invalid Markdown-relative paths are not included in the thesis source.

The low-mode idea remains sound for measurement design because spatial-mode structure contains useful sub-Rayleigh information.  However, the available record cannot support the jump from one aggregate population count to a reduced experimental quantum state.  The sequential channel framework makes this limit clear while keeping the useful result.  It provides a method for fitting the identifiable population-level channel, separates conditional throughput from efficiency, and gives a modular path for information-loss calculations.  Complete channel reconstruction and experimental QFI will require the additional modal, flux, failure, and coherence-sensitive measurements listed in Table~\ref{tab:spade_calibration_handoff}.
