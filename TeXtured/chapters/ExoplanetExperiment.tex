\chapter{SPADE as a Quantum-to-Classical Channel}
\label{ch:exoplanet_experiment}

Chapter~\ref{ch:astrophysical_theory} introduced SPADE.  This project began with the aim of applying the truncated-QFI methods of Chapter~\ref{ch:methods} to experimental SPADE data provided by \textbf{ASI Matera}~\cite{Santamaria2024single}.  The dataset became available only after the experiment had ended, so the extra measurements needed for state reconstruction could not be added.  The recorded data contain one aggregate first-order count for each repetition, but not the separate modal outputs and coherence-sensitive measurements needed to build a reduced optical state for an experimental TQFI calculation.

This limitation led to a change in perspective.  First, a classical statistical model was fitted to determine which features of the measured counts could be predicted reliably.  The experiment was then described as a sequential quantum-to-classical channel model.  This model separates the parameterized source state, quantum propagation, accessible-mode reduction, measurement, classical readout, calibration, and count process.  It also states which parameters are fitted, fixed by external calibration, assumed, or not identifiable.  The data constrain only the population-level projection, meaning the classical probabilities or diagonal density-matrix entries, while the assumed quantum model provides checkpoints for conditional QFI and CFI calculations.

The resulting framework is foundation work for future SPADE experiments.  It shows how a practical channel model can be fitted without claiming that the available counts reconstruct the full quantum channel.  It also provides a clear basis for possible future collaboration with ASI Matera.  A new experiment could be planned from the start to record the separate modal, flux, failure, and coherence-sensitive measurements required for an experimental link between SPADE and TQFI.

\begin{remark}[Central conclusion of this chapter]
    The quantum-channel framework provides a physically complete and modular description of the experiment and enables conditional QFI calculations.  The current aggregate-count data constrain only its population-level classical projection.  They do not reconstruct the complete quantum channel or its coherence dynamics.
\end{remark}



\section{SPADE integration into TQFI formalism}
\label{sec:spade_question_record}

\subsection{The low-mode link to TQFI}

The connection to TQFI comes from the low-order SPADE receiver in Chapter~\ref{ch:astrophysical_theory} and the rank-truncation methods in Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}.  A displaced point-spread function has support in an infinite-dimensional HG space, although its lowest orders carry most of the displacement response near alignment.  Relating these measured modes to TQFI requires three different reductions to be kept separate.

\paragraph{Numerical HG cutoff.}
A numerical cutoff approximates the infinite-dimensional source model by keeping HG modes up to a stated order.  Its accuracy is checked by increasing the cutoff until the calculated quantities converge.  It is a computational approximation, not a restriction imposed by the apparatus.

\paragraph{Accessible-mode channel.}
The apparatus gives access to a fixed set of modes.  A physical reduction to this set must keep the probability outside it as a complement or failure outcome.  For a selected subspace with projector $P_M$, we use the following flagged channel.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_M(\rho)
    =P_M\rho P_M
    +\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(\mathbb{I}-P_M)\rho\right]
    |\mathrm{fail}\rangle\langle\mathrm{fail}|
    \label{eq:spade_accessible_projection}
\end{equation}
The failure flag keeps the probability in unobserved modes.  Discarding this probability and renormalizing $P_M\rho P_M$ would introduce postselection and could overstate the available information.

\paragraph{Rank-$m$ TQFI.}
Rank-$m$ TQFI instead keeps the $m$ dominant eigenvectors of the parameterized density operator.  These state-dependent eigenvectors do not have to match the HG modes selected by the apparatus.  This reduction therefore requires a reconstructed parameterized state rather than a chosen set of detector ports.


\subsection{Dataset description: the count \enquote{cube}}

The recorded observable is:
\begin{equation}
    n_1=n_{HG_{01}}+n_{HG_{10}}
    \qquad
    \texttt{H\_data}[d_a,\epsilon,r]=n_{1,r}
    \label{eq:spade_recorded_count}
\end{equation}
Here, $d_a=d/w_0$ is the signed nominal displacement, $\epsilon$ is the faint-source fraction defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:intensity_ratio_epsilon}, and $r$ indexes repetitions.  The cube contains $21\times25\times100=52{,}500$ integer counts, with $d_a\in[-0.66667,0.66667]$, $\epsilon\in[0.00427,0.09593]$, and $100$ independent $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ repetitions at every setting, see Figure~\ref{fig:spade_count_data}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.43\textwidth]{figures/asi_report/sample_1_exoplanet_2026.png}
    \includegraphics[width=0.52\textwidth]{figures/asi_report/average_sample_exoplanet_2026.png}
    \caption{Sample and average SPADE count data for the exoplanet experiment. The left panel shows a single repetition, while the right panel shows the average over all repetitions.}
    \label{fig:spade_count_data}
\end{figure}

The count cube provides the aggregate $n_1$ at every nominal $(d_a,\epsilon)$ setting, the signed scan coordinates, and the programmed source fractions.  Its repetitions support estimates of setting means, variances, and empirical uncertainties.  It does not include simultaneous $HG_{00}$ counts, separate $HG_{01}$ and $HG_{10}$ streams, higher-order modes, the complement, or no-click events.  Incident-flux monitoring, source-blocked background, timestamps, detector diagnostics, and a complete mode-injection transfer matrix are also absent.  Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics} gives the detailed raw-count diagnostics and exposure checks.

The missing outcomes set the limit of the analysis.  Separate $HG_{01}$ and $HG_{10}$ counts would resolve two orthogonal first-order directions, while $HG_{00}$ together with aggregate $HG_1$ would provide a reference and a separation-sensitive outcome.  The record contains neither pair.  A nonzero $n_1$ at $d_a=0$ can also combine background, bright-mode leakage, coupling variation, alignment error, and higher-order leakage.  Dividing $n_1$ by an assumed photon number and setting $p_0=1-p_1$ would hide these contributions in the remainder.  Since the data contain no phase-sensitive observable, they also cannot determine off-diagonal HG coherences.  The record can test forward count models, but experimental TQFI requires additional modal and coherence-sensitive measurements.

\section{The Sequential Quantum-to-Classical Fitting Method}
\label{sec:spade_sequential_channels}
% ==============================================================================

We use the density-operator, quantum-channel, and POVM conventions introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}, with supplementary generalized-measurement details collected in Appendix~\ref{app:mathematical_background}. Here we specify only the physical action and experiment-specific parameters of each channel.

The complete ordering is
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small]
    source preparation
    -> survival / erasure
    -> displacement and sorter alignment
    -> possible HG-basis dephasing
    -> possible coherent HG-mode mixing
    -> mode-dependent loss into a failure sector
    -> flagged accessible-mode reduction
    -> imperfect / limited-outcome SPADE measurement
    -> classical readout / cross-talk
    -> background and calibration variation
    -> discrete repeated-count observation
\end{Verbatim}

This ordering also classifies every stage.  The source mixture is a quantum state preparation.  Survival, alignment, dephasing, coherent mixing, modal loss, and accessible-mode reduction are quantum channels.  SPADE is the quantum measurement that produces classical outcome probabilities.  Readout confusion is a classical stochastic channel.  Background and calibration variation form a classical calibration layer, while repeated counts form a classical statistical model.  Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics} lists the parameter source and information role of every stage.

\subsection{Classical and quantum objects in the model}

A classical state is a probability vector $\boldsymbol p$.  In a chosen basis, it may be embedded as a diagonal density matrix.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{diag}}=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol p)
    \label{eq:spade_classical_embedding}
\end{equation}
A general quantum state can also contain off-diagonal coherences.  A classical stochastic channel maps probability vectors to probability vectors.  A quantum channel acts on the complete density operator and can describe both populations and coherences.  Measurement produces a classical vector $p_y=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho M_y)$.  Detector reporting and repeated-count statistics then act on that vector.  A probability vector is not a quantum channel or a substitute for the pre-measurement state.

The main advantage of the quantum description is not the number of adjustable parameters.  It is the ability to represent physically meaningful coherence, interference, loss, mode mixing, and open-system dynamics while preserving positivity and normalization.  The classical layers then describe what happens after measurement without treating detector effects as changes to the pre-measurement quantum state.


\subsection{Source preparation and the HG convention}

The spatial one-photon sector is not limited to two dimensions.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{spatial}}
    =\operatorname{span}\{|HG_{00}\rangle,|HG_{01}\rangle,|HG_{10}\rangle,|HG_{02}\rangle,\ldots\}
    \label{eq:spade_infinite_hg_space}
\end{equation}
For a one-photon event before apparatus loss, we use the following mutually incoherent bright/faint source state.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_S(d_a,\epsilon)
    =(1-\epsilon)|HG_{00}\rangle\langle HG_{00}|
    +\epsilon|\psi_{d_a}\rangle\langle\psi_{d_a}|
    \label{eq:spade_source_state}
\end{equation}
The sorter origin is aligned to the bright source, as in the experiment~\cite{Santamaria2024single}.  For the scan direction, the displaced state has the following expansion.
\begin{equation}
    |\psi_{d_a}\rangle
    =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-q/2}\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|HG_n\rangle
    \qquad
    \alpha=\frac{d_a}{2}
    \qquad
    q=|\alpha|^2=\frac{d_a^2}{4}
    \label{eq:spade_effective_coordinates}
\end{equation}
The modal probabilities follow from this expansion.
\begin{equation}
    P(HG_n|\psi_{d_a})=e^{-q}\frac{q^n}{n!}
    \qquad
    p_1(d_a,\epsilon)=\epsilon q e^{-q}
    \label{eq:spade_sector_probabilities}
\end{equation}
Here $p_1$ is the ideal aggregate first-order probability for the one-axis displacement model used by the count analysis.

Even if $|\psi_{d_a}\rangle$ has nonzero amplitudes in infinitely many HG modes, the one-photon state in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spade_source_state} is a mixture of at most two pure states.  Its rank is therefore at most two.  Infinite basis support and density-matrix rank are different concepts.  The numerical cutoff check is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Pre-measurement quantum channels}

\paragraph{Survival or erasure.}
For a one-photon input, the following enlarged-space model represents parameter-independent survival.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{surv}}
    =\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\rho_S)
    =\eta\rho_S+(1-\eta)|\mathrm{vac}\rangle\langle\mathrm{vac}|
    \label{eq:spade_loss_channel}
\end{equation}

This quantum erasure channel represents propagation, collection, and detection survival when these processes occur before the modelled measurement.  The dataset does not measure its parameter $\eta$ because it does not record the number $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ of photons per acquisition.

\paragraph{Alignment and displacement.}
A fixed displacement of the sorter basis is a parameter-independent unitary channel.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{align}}
    =U_{\mathrm{align}}(\delta_{\mathrm{sorter}})
    \rho_{\mathrm{surv}}
    U_{\mathrm{align}}^{\dagger}(\delta_{\mathrm{sorter}})
    \label{eq:spade_alignment_channel}
\end{equation}
It preserves state QFI, but it can reduce the CFI of a fixed downstream SPADE basis.  The fitted $\delta$ in the empirical mean model is an effective displacement and calibration offset.  It is not an independent measurement of $\delta_{\mathrm{sorter}}$.  Random displacement or pointing jitter is instead a mixture over displacement unitaries and can reduce coherence.  Its strength is not identified by the current data, so it is used only as a provisional sensitivity test in Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics}.

\paragraph{HG-basis dephasing.}
The following channel represents possible phase scrambling between HG amplitudes.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{v}(\rho)
    =v\rho+(1-v)\operatorname{diag}_{HG}(\rho)
    \qquad 0\leq v\leq1
    \label{eq:spade_quantum_sequence}
\end{equation}
This quantum channel suppresses off-diagonal HG coherences while keeping every modal population unchanged.  It may change state QFI, but the current diagonal aggregate measurement is exactly insensitive to $v$.

\paragraph{Coherent mode mixing.}
Coherent coupling of $HG_0$ and $HG_1$ is represented by a unitary rotation with a mixing angle and phase.  If these parameters are independent of $d_a$, the channel preserves state QFI while changing the CFI reached by a fixed SPADE measurement.  The current counts do not identify the angle or phase.

\paragraph{Mode-dependent loss.}
If mode $j$ has transmission $\tau_j$, the operator $L=\sum_j\sqrt{\tau_j}|HG_j\rangle\langle HG_j|$ attenuates the surviving block.  The following map places the missing probability in an orthogonal flag.

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol\tau}(\rho)
    =L\rho L^{\dagger}
    +\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(\mathbb{I}-L^{\dagger}L)\rho\right]
    |\mathrm{fail}\rangle\langle\mathrm{fail}|
    \label{eq:spade_modal_loss}
\end{equation}

This construction preserves probability in the enlarged space.  Mode-dependent transmission can affect accessible-state QFI and measurement CFI.  The current record contains no absolute transmission for each mode.  Any values of $\tau_j$ are therefore provisional sensitivity assumptions, which are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics}.  The accessible-mode channel in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spade_accessible_projection} is applied after these pre-measurement maps.  It also keeps its complement instead of renormalizing it away.

\subsection{Measurement and post-measurement classical layers}

\paragraph{SPADE measurement.}
Using the POVM convention of Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}, we specify the ideal low-order outcomes below.
\begin{align}
    M_0               & =|HG_{00}\rangle\langle HG_{00}|\nonumber \\
    M_1               & =|HG_{01}\rangle\langle HG_{01}|
    +|HG_{10}\rangle\langle HG_{10}|\nonumber                     \\
    M_{\mathrm{fail}} & =\mathbb{I}-M_0-M_1
    \label{eq:spade_imperfect_povm}
\end{align}
The accessible state gives probabilities $p_y=\operatorname{Tr}(M_y\rho_M)$.  A complete low-order experiment would record all three outcomes and ideally record the two first-order ports separately.  In the practical model, the pre-measurement quantum channels contain optical mixing and collection loss.  Coarse-graining of the POVM represents limited outcome resolution.  The available file stores only repeated counts assigned to the coarse-grained $M_1$ result.

\paragraph{Classical readout and cross-talk.}
Once the POVM has produced the probability vector $\boldsymbol p$, with components $p_0$, $p_1$, and $p_{\mathrm{fail}}$, reported-port confusion is a classical stochastic channel $\boldsymbol p_{\mathrm{rep}}=R\boldsymbol p$.  The identifiable sequential fit uses the readout matrix below.
\begin{equation}
    R_{\chi}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1-\chi & \chi   & 0 \\
        \chi   & 1-\chi & 0 \\
        0      & 0      & 1
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad \chi=0.0035
    \label{eq:spade_readout_matrix}
\end{equation}
The value $\chi=0.0035$ is fixed externally from the paper's reported cross-talk calibration~\cite{Santamaria2024single}.  The paper does not give a complete measured transfer matrix.  The symmetric matrix and the isolation of the failure outcome are therefore assumptions.  This classical map can reduce measurement CFI, but it does not change pre-measurement QFI.

\paragraph{Calibration, background, and counts.}
In the final stages, an additive background $B$, a multiplicative coupling field $g(d_a,\epsilon)$, and a discrete repeated-count law act on the reported probability.  We use the following sequential mean.
\begin{equation}
    \mu(d_a,\epsilon)
    =B+T\,g(d_a,\epsilon)\,p_{\mathrm{rep},1}(d_a,\epsilon)
    \qquad
    T=N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta
    \label{eq:spade_count_channel}
\end{equation}
Here $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ denotes the incident photons in one $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ acquisition.  The parameter $\eta$ denotes their survival probability under the stated pre-measurement convention.  The product $T$ is the identifiable conditional throughput scale.  A repeated count is then written as $n_{1,r}\sim P_{\mathrm{count}}(\mu,\boldsymbol\zeta)$, where $\boldsymbol\zeta$ contains classical dispersion or tail parameters.  Background, calibration variation, and the count law may change final count statistics and count CFI.  They do not change the state QFI upstream of the POVM.

The mean also shows the main quantities that cannot be separated.  The signal constrains products such as $N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta$ and modal transmission more directly than their separate factors.  The baseline can trade optical leakage against additive background.  A good fit to $\mu$ cannot uniquely validate all pre-measurement quantum maps.

\subsection{Why the channel formulation is useful}

The channel model preserves positivity and total probability.  It sends lost or inaccessible light to vacuum or a failure flag.  It also separates modal populations from coherences, so dephasing and interference can be represented even though the current counts cannot determine them.  Optical loss, alignment, mode mixing, readout cross-talk, background, calibration variation, and count noise are therefore kept as distinct physical or statistical effects.

This separation gives clear information checkpoints.  Source-state QFI, post-channel QFI, accessible-state QFI, SPADE CFI, readout CFI, and final count CFI can be compared without mixing per-photon and per-acquisition quantities.  It also exposes identifiable combinations such as $T=N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta$.  Each parameter has a stated source, and a better calibration can replace one part of the model without changing the whole inference and QFI pipeline.

The same structure guides future experiments.  A flux monitor, a complete mode-injection matrix, a recorded failure outcome, or a coherence-sensitive receiver can be linked directly to the channel that it would constrain.

For parameter-independent maps, the matching information checkpoints follow the data-processing order shown below.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_Q(\rho_S)
    \geq \mathcal{F}_Q(\rho_{\mathrm{post}})
    \geq \mathcal{F}_Q[\mathcal{E}_M(\rho_{\mathrm{post}})]
    \geq \mathcal{F}_{C}(\text{SPADE})
    \geq \mathcal{F}_{C}(\text{readout})
    \label{eq:spade_information_processing_chain}
\end{equation}
Equality holds across a fixed unitary at the state-QFI checkpoint.  Per-surviving-photon quantities become per-acquisition resources only after multiplication by a justified photon number or throughput.  The behavior of a stochastic calibration field during an unknown-source measurement must also be specified before a final count CFI can be defined.

\subsection{Limitations of the formulation}

Adding channel parameters does not by itself make a model more scientifically valid.  Coherence parameters cannot be inferred from diagonal aggregate counts, and several quantum channels can have the same population-level projection.  A good fit of final count means does not uniquely validate the full channel sequence.  Invented or weakly constrained values must not be presented as experimental measurements.  In particular, the current record provides neither quantum process tomography nor a directly reconstructed experimental QFI.  These limits explain the clear provenance labels used in Sections~\ref{sec:spade_conditional_inference} and~\ref{sec:spade_qfi_role}.

\section{Evidence from the Recorded Counts}
\label{sec:spade_data_diagnostics}

The raw setting means range from $6.93$ to $19.44$ counts per $10\,\mathrm{ms}$.  The median mean at zero separation is $8.17$, and the median Fano factor is $2.497$.  The data therefore show a nonzero baseline, super-Poissonian variation, and structured setting dependence.  They do not identify a unique cause for these effects.

Each setting contains $100$ independent $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ repetitions.  The paper's normalization of about $40{,}000$--$80{,}000$ detected photons applies to their one-second aggregate, not to one stored repetition.  Incident photons, detected photons, first-order counts, and acquisition duration must therefore remain separate.

The low setting means also make a Gaussian count model unsuitable for the main analysis.  NB1 and Poisson--lognormal diagnostics preserve the discrete observations, but neither one-parameter family reproduces all the observed variance and tail structure.  Uncertainty at recorded settings is therefore propagated by resampling the observed repetitions.

A fixed paper-inspired benchmark and three more flexible deterministic mean surfaces fail refitted discrete-count bootstrap checks.  The extra terms improve the visual mean fit but do not give a valid model for individual counts.  Boundary-hitting PSF scales and a negative fitted tail coefficient show that these terms absorb missing calibration structure.  The negative coefficient is not a physical leakage probability.  Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics} gives the exposure calculation, fit results, figures, and bootstrap diagnostics.

\section{The Predictively Validated Mean Channel}
\label{sec:spade_conditional_inference}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Hierarchical calibration model}

The main positive result uses the physical source response as its central mean.
\begin{equation}
    \mu_0(d_a,\epsilon)
    =B+A\epsilon q e^{-q}
    \qquad
    q=\frac{(d_a-\delta)^2}{4}
    \label{eq:spade_effective_count_model}
\end{equation}
Here, $\delta$ is a dimensionless offset in units of $w_0$.  The model uses the bright-source-aligned convention $q=d_{\mathrm{eff}}^2/4$ with $d_{\mathrm{eff}}=d_a-\delta$.
The following relation describes the remaining response variation.
\begin{equation}
    \log\!\frac{\mu(d_a,\epsilon)}{\mu_0(d_a,\epsilon)}
    \sim \text{Mat\'ern-$3/2$ Gaussian process}
    +\text{independent setting scatter}
    \label{eq:spade_calibration_gp}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian process is a \emph{classical calibration channel} placed after the ideal optical response.  It represents coupling or calibration variation.  It is not a new quantum effect or source-encoded separation information.

Grouped five-fold validation holds out checkerboard cells, complete distance rows, and complete source-ratio columns.  Across these tests, the standardized residual standard deviation is about $1.00$--$1.04$, and nominal $95\%$ coverage is about $93.5\%$--$94.7\%$.  The model therefore predicts setting means even when complete structures are missing from the fit.

This result applies to the mean of $100$ repeated observations at each setting.  It does not validate a complete distribution for every $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ count, make photon survival identifiable, or reconstruct quantum coherence.  Empirical repeat resampling remains suitable for uncertainty estimates at observed settings.  Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics} gives the fitted calibration scales, fold-by-fold results, and diagnostic figure.

\subsection{The identifiable sequential-channel scale}

The sequential fit combines the source, the flagged $HG_0/HG_1$ reduction, the externally fixed readout in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spade_readout_matrix}, a fitted background, a zero-mean coupling field, and an empirical repeated-count layer.  The zero weighted mean convention prevents the coupling field from absorbing the global scale.  Since the file contains neither launched photons nor an absolute $HG_1$ coupling, the main quantity that can be estimated is the conditional throughput.
\begin{equation}
    T=N_{\mathrm{in}}\eta
    \label{eq:spade_effective_parameter_combinations}
\end{equation}
The calibrated result is $T=1304.25$ per $10\,\mathrm{ms}$, with a grouped cross-fit envelope of $[1251.49,1359.47]$.  This is a conditional throughput scale under the fixed readout and zero-mean coupling conventions.  It is not an absolute efficiency.  Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics} gives the nuisance estimates, resampling interval, and grouped validation details.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/exoplanets/spade_identifiable_quantum_channel.png}
    \caption{Identifiable sequential-channel fit.  The panels show observed and calibrated setting means, in-sample residuals, the assumed symmetric readout matrix, held-out checkerboard prediction, and the spread of conditional throughput under grouped cross-fitting.  The result is conditional on the fixed readout and zero-mean coupling convention.  It identifies a throughput scale, not an absolute efficiency.}
    \label{fig:spade_identifiable_channel}
\end{figure}

An independent launched-photon measurement would convert this scale into $\eta=1304.25/N_{\mathrm{in,10\,ms}}$.  Without that measurement, $T$ and $\eta$ must remain distinct.  Background and cross-talk also trade against one another and cannot be separated from the aggregate stream.

Random displacement, dephasing, coherent mixing, modal loss, and asymmetric readout are kept in a replaceable sensitivity workbench.  Their example values were not inferred from this dataset.  Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics} lists every value and its source.  A future measured calibration can replace a provisional value without changing the channel order, fitting method, or QFI pipeline.

\section{Conditional Information After the Channels}
\label{sec:spade_qfi_role}
% ==============================================================================

The definitions and numerical methods are those of Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}.  No information quantity is calculated by substituting empirical count variance into a QFI formula.  For the specified channel parameters $\boldsymbol\lambda$, the model quantity used here is defined below.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_{Q}^{\mathrm{model}}(d_a)
    =\mathcal{F}_{Q}\!\left[
        \rho_{\mathrm{post}}(d_a,\epsilon;\boldsymbol\lambda)
        \right]
    \label{eq:spade_model_qfi}
\end{equation}
It is evaluated from the assumed parameterized pre-measurement state for estimation of $d_a$.  Measurement CFI is then evaluated after the stated SPADE and readout maps.  These calculations show where a proposed instrument can lose information.  They do not reconstruct experimental QFI from $n_1$.

\subsection{Conditional information retention}

All values below concern estimation of $d_a$.  They are fractions of source-state QFI per surviving photon, not per acquisition.  The full ideal-SPADE and first-order binary results use twelve regular nonzero settings from the measured grid.  The accessible-state and readout results use the nine-point ladder listed in Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics}.  The rank-boundary point $d_a=0$ is excluded from these regular-point ranges.

Full ideal SPADE keeps $0.99899$--$1.00000$ of the source-state QFI in the assumed source model.  The ideal first-order binary statistic keeps $0.78434$--$0.94485$.  After the flagged $HG_0/HG_1$ accessible-mode channel, the state QFI remains at $0.99777$--$1.00000$, while the resolved accessible-outcome CFI remains at $0.99681$--$1.00000$.  Binary readout after the assumed symmetric confusion with $\chi=0.0035$ keeps only $0.00134$--$0.57026$.

These theoretical and conditional calculations show that full ideal SPADE is nearly QFI-optimal over the tested settings.  The flagged accessible channel loses little because it keeps the complement.  By contrast, binary aggregation and assumed bright-mode leakage can remove much of the usable CFI near zero separation.  Appendix~\ref{app:spade_diagnostics} gives the setting grids, numerical checks, and comparison table.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/exoplanets/spade_information_retention.png}
    \caption{Theoretical information kept by the bright-source-aligned source model.  The curves show full ideal-SPADE CFI/QFI and ideal first-order binary CFI/QFI versus $|d|/w_0$ at three source fractions.  The grey region marks the measured separation range.  Full ideal SPADE remains nearly optimal, while first-order information decreases at larger separation.  These per-surviving-photon curves contain no fitted final-count channel and are not an experimental QFI reconstruction.}

No final noisy-count CFI is reported.  Calculating it would require a rule for how the fitted calibration field behaves during an unknown-source acquisition and a validated distribution for individual counts.  Good prediction of the setting means alone does not provide either one.

\section{Supported Conclusions and Measurement Priorities}
\label{sec:spade_conclusions}
% ==============================================================================

\begin{remark}[Scope of the result]
    The hierarchical classical calibration channel predicts held-out $100$-repeat setting means.  Under the stated conventions, the identifiable experimental scale is the conditional throughput $T$, not the efficiency $\eta$.  Full ideal SPADE is nearly QFI-optimal for the assumed source model.  The counts do not reconstruct a density matrix, quantum coherence, the complete channel, or an experimental QFI.
\end{remark}

The most useful next step is a new experiment designed around these limits, possibly in renewed collaboration with ASI Matera.  Separate, time-stamped $HG_{01}$ and $HG_{10}$ streams would resolve directional response and asymmetry.  Simultaneous or interleaved $HG_{00}$ counts, selected higher modes, and a complement or no-click outcome would give modal normalization and preserve the probability outside the measured subspace.

An incident-flux monitor would separate $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ from $\eta$.  Source-blocked runs and detector diagnostics would constrain background and detector effects.  Single-mode injection into every relevant input would determine the readout matrix and modal transmissions.  Independent calibration of $w_0$, $\epsilon$, source position, and sorter alignment would separate nominal coordinates from physical offsets.  Finally, phase-sensitive modal measurements would be needed to constrain coherences, dephasing, and coherent transfer.

With these additions, the experimentally measured modal probabilities would first support the CFI of the actual SPADE receiver.  Independent coherence-sensitive data or a separately validated channel would then support comparison with state QFI or TQFI.  This ordering avoids treating population counts as quantum tomography.
