\chapter{Methods: Variational Estimation of Mixed-State QFI} \label{ch:methods}

This chapter develops the computational methodology used to estimate the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) for mixed states. Exact QFI provides a useful benchmark, but its evaluation requires operations on the full density matrix and becomes computationally expensive as the system size increases. So, we consider fidelity-based bounds that can be evaluated from the dominant components or trace functionals of the state.

We define as a toy model a one-dimensional Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM), that can be considered as our controlled magnetometer. Reduced mixed states are generated by tracing out part of an extended probe, and their exact QFI is computed. This setting makes it possible to compare exact calculations with the Truncated Quantum Fisher Information (TQFI) and sub- and super-fidelity QFI (SSQFI) bounds.

Then we develop the variational implementation of these bounds. We implement the quantum circuit for a Variational Quantum State Eigensolver (VQSE) to identify the dominant eigenspace required by TQFI and place it within the Variational Quantum Fisher Information Estimation (VQFIE) pipeline~\cite{beckey2022vqfiea,cerezo2022vqse}.

This builds a quantum--classical procedure that estimates and optimizes mixed-state metrological bounds without full density-matrix diagonalization. We also make a mathematical twin of the quantum circuits, using classical linear algebra and quantum simulations, to be able to validate and benchmark the variational procedures. The TFIM construction, exact benchmark, variational algorithms, and required circuit primitives presented here provide the methodological basis for the numerical analysis of Chapter~\ref{ch:noise_analysis}.

\section{Physical Toy Model: Open 1-D TFIM}
% ==============================================================================

We model the quantum sensor as a 1D $N$-qubit Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM). The external parameter to be estimated is the transverse magnetic field strength, so $\theta\equiv h_x$. After preparation and evolution, only the smaller $n$-qubit subsystem is kept as the accessible probe.

\begin{definition}[Transverse-Field Ising Hamiltonian]
    The Hamiltonian governing the total $N$-qubit system is defined as:
    \begin{equation}
        H_N(h_x) = -J \sum_{i=0}^{N-2} Z_i Z_{i+1} - h_x \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} X_i
        \label{eq:tfim_hamiltonian}
    \end{equation}
    where $J>0$ is the ferromagnetic nearest-neighbor Ising coupling, $h_x$ is the field applied along the transverse $x$ direction, and $X_i$ and $Z_i$ are Pauli operators acting on site $i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Quantum Phase Transition and Ground States]
    The 1D TFIM is a standard benchmark for quantum metrology because it exhibits a quantum phase transition at $h_x = J$. This criticality separates two distinct physical regimes characterized by their macroscopic ground states:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Ferromagnetic Phase ($h_x < J$):} The nearest-neighbor coupling dominates. The spins align along the longitudinal $z$-axis, driving the system toward a doubly degenerate ground state of either all spins up ($|0\rangle^{\otimes N}$) or all spins down ($|1\rangle^{\otimes N}$).
        \item \textbf{Paramagnetic Phase ($h_x > J$):} The transverse field dominates. The individual spins are forced to align independently along the transverse $x$-axis, driving the system toward a unique symmetric product state ($|+\rangle^{\otimes N}$).
    \end{itemize}
    At the critical point $h_x = J$, the system is really sensitive to small changes, that can easily break the symmetry.
\end{remark}
\subsection{State Initialization and Mixed-State Generation via Partial Trace}
For the circuit-level dynamical construction, we initialize the total $N$-qubit system in the reference product state $|0\rangle^{\otimes N}$ and we evolve it under the unitary dynamics generated by $H_N(h_x)$. The resulting pure state depends on the field through
\begin{equation}
    |\Psi_\theta(t)\rangle=U_\theta(t)|0\rangle^{\otimes N},
    \qquad
    U_\theta(t)=e^{-iH_N(\theta)t}
    \label{eq:tfim_unitary_preparation}
\end{equation}


We keep the $n$-qubit subsystem $S=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ and trace out the complementary subsystem $E=\{n,\ldots,N-1\}$, discarding $N-n$ qubits (See Figure~\ref{fig:tfim_trotter_circuits}).

\begin{definition}[Reduced Probe State Generation]
    The exact parameter-dependent mixed state $\rho_\theta$ acting on the retained $n$-qubit sensor subspace $\mathcal{H}_S$ is generated by tracing out all $N-n$ qubits in $E$ from the evolved pure state:
    \begin{equation}
        \rho_\theta = \operatorname{Tr}_{E} \left[ U_\theta \left( |0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes N} \right) U_\theta^\dagger \right],
        \qquad E=\{n,\ldots,N-1\}
        \label{eq:partial_trace_state}
    \end{equation}
    Similarly, the shifted state $\rho_{\theta+\delta}$ is generated under the perturbed magnetic field $h_x + \delta$:
    \begin{equation}
        \rho_{\theta+\delta} = \operatorname{Tr}_{E} \left[ U_{\theta+\delta} \left( |0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes N} \right) U_{\theta+\delta}^\dagger \right]
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Participation Ratio and Effective Rank]
    The partial trace integrates out the $N-n$ inaccessible degrees of freedom and generally transforms the total pure state into a mixed state on the retained subsystem. The degree of mixing is quantified by the participation ratio $R = 1 / \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_\theta^2)$, which indicates the effective number of dominant eigenvalues required for accurate rank truncation. Trivially if it is pure we only need one eigenvalue since $\rho = \ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{infobox}{First-Order Suzuki--Trotter Approximation}
    \label{sec:trotterization}

    The coupling $ZZ$ and field $X$ contributions,
    \begin{equation}
        H_{ZZ}=-J\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}Z_iZ_{i+1},
        \qquad
        H_X=-h_x\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}X_i
    \end{equation}
    do not commute. Their combined evolution therefore cannot be implemented on a circuit exactly as one coupling layer followed by one field layer.

    A first-order Suzuki--Trotter decomposition with $r$ steps approximates it as~\cite{hatano_2005_suzuki_trotter}
    \begin{equation}
        e^{-it(H_{ZZ}+H_X)}
        =\left(e^{-i(t/r)H_{ZZ}}e^{-i(t/r)H_X}\right)^r
        +\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{t^2}{r}\right)
        \label{eq:first_order_trotter}
    \end{equation}
    Classical numerical simulations can compute the exact global evolution directly via matrix exponentiation, so it is not necessary for the simulations.
    Instead, Trotterization is required on a physical quantum circuit to decompose the continuous dynamics into discrete, realizable gate operations that approximate the exact simulated evolution. Increasing $r$ reduces the error of this circuit approximation, but at the cost of increased gate depth (See Figure~\ref{fig:tfim_trotter_circuits}).
\end{infobox}

\subsection{Ground-State and Gibbs Preparations}
The unitary construction above illustrates the general idea of the protocol: prepare the complete probe, encode $h_x$, evolve, and only then restrict access to part of the probe, tracing out $E$. This can enable us to work with a controlled mixed state: we know the generator, we know the original parameter encoded, and we know the evolution.

\subsubsection{Ground-State Preparation}
For each value of $h_x$, we find the lowest-energy eigenstate of $H_N(h_x)$. We denote this ground state by $|\psi_0(h_x)\rangle$ and its energy by $E_0(h_x)$:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{G},N}(h_x)
    =|\psi_0(h_x)\rangle\langle\psi_0(h_x)|,
    \qquad
    H_N(h_x)|\psi_0(h_x)\rangle=E_0(h_x)|\psi_0(h_x)\rangle
\end{equation}
The complete ground state is pure, whereas the observer has access only to
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{G},S}(h_x)
    =\operatorname{Tr}_E[\rho_{\mathrm{G},N}(h_x)]
\end{equation}
which may be mixed because of entanglement between $S$ and $E$.

\subsubsection{Gibbs Preparation}
At finite temperature, the probe can also occupy excited energy levels. We describe this equilibrium preparation with the Gibbs state
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{th},N}(h_x;\beta)
    =\frac{e^{-\beta H_N(h_x)}}{Z(h_x,\beta)},
    \qquad
    Z(h_x,\beta)=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[e^{-\beta H_N(h_x)}\right]
    \label{eq:methods_gibbs_preparation}
\end{equation}
The partition function $Z(h_x,\beta)$ normalizes the state so that its trace is one. The inverse temperature is $\beta=(k_{\mathrm B}T)^{-1}$. So a larger $\beta$ describes a colder state, while a smaller $\beta$ gives more weight to excited states. Unlike the ground state, the complete Gibbs state is generally mixed before any qubits are traced out. For a nondegenerate ground state, it approaches the ground state as $\beta\rightarrow\infty$.

After preparing the complete Gibbs state, we obtain the state available to the observer by tracing out $E$:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{th},S}(h_x;\beta)
    =\operatorname{Tr}_E[\rho_{\mathrm{th},N}(h_x;\beta)]
\end{equation}
The Gibbs state is prepared separately for every value of $h_x$. Changing the field changes the energy levels, their eigenstates, and their thermal populations. Therefore, Gibbs preparation is not a fixed noise channel applied to the ground state. It is a parameter-dependent family of states defined directly by the Hamiltonian.

\subsubsection{Additional Noise}
The ground-state and Gibbs constructions specify how the probe is prepared. A separate physical disturbance is represented by a quantum channel $\Lambda_\eta^{(N)}$. The Kraus representation of a quantum channel is introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}, and its equivalent Choi-matrix representation is summarized in Appendix~\ref{sec:choi_quantum_channel}. But the role of this channel in the protocol is that it acts on the prepared complete state before $E$ is discarded:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{noise},S}(h_x;\eta)
    =\operatorname{Tr}_E\!\left[
        \Lambda_\eta^{(N)}\!\left(\rho_{\mathrm{prep},N}(h_x)\right)
        \right]
    \label{eq:methods_generic_noise_channel}
\end{equation}
The parameter $\eta$ controls the noise strength. When $\eta$ is fixed, the same noise map is applied for every value of $h_x$. This is what we mean by a parameter-independent channel. Chapter~\ref{ch:noise_analysis} defines the depolarizing channel used in the calculations and compares its effect with the ground-state and Gibbs preparations. Basically it has a probability of replacing the state with the maximally mixed state.

\section{Exact Baseline: Ground-Truth QFI Evaluation}
% ==============================================================================

To establish a numerical ground truth against which variational approximations can be benchmarked, we compute the exact QFI of the reduced state $\rho_\theta$. The SLD definition and spectral expression are given in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:sld_lyapunov} and \eqref{eq:qfi_spectral_explicit}.
For the small reference systems we also use the vectorized QFIM formula in Eq.~\eqref{eq:safranek_vectorized}.

\subsection{Numerical Differentiation and Exact Benchmarking}
When explicit analytical derivatives of the reduced density matrix $\rho_\theta$ are intractable, we approximate the parameter derivative $\partial_\theta \rho_\theta$ in our numerical pipeline using a second-order central difference:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \rho_\theta}{\partial \theta} \approx \frac{\rho_{\theta+\delta} - \rho_{\theta-\delta}}{2\delta} + \mathcal{O}(\delta^2)
    \label{eq:central_difference}
\end{equation}

Evaluating the exact SLD matrix elements and QFI requires full classical diagonalization of the $2^n \times 2^n$ density matrix, scaling as $\mathcal{O}(8^n)$. This classical bottleneck motivates the variational bounds implemented in this chapter.

\section{Dynamics-Agnostic Bounds: TQFI and SSQFI} \label{sec:bounds}
% ==============================================================================

On near-term hardware, exact diagonalization is intractable. To address this bottleneck, we implement dynamics-agnostic lower and upper bounds derived from the fidelity $F(\rho_\theta, \rho_{\theta+\delta})$. Its connection to QFI is given in Eqs.~\eqref{def:bures_distance} and~\eqref{def:qfim_bures_relation} and developed for VQFIE in~\cite{beckey2022vqfiea}. Here $F$ denotes the root-fidelity convention used in that construction. We denote the finite-displacement functional by $\mathcal{I}_\delta(\theta;\rho_\theta)$ and use $\mathcal{F}_Q(\theta)$ just for its infinitesimal limit $\delta\to0$. They are dynamic agnostic because, differently than the SLD, they do not require knowledge of the generator of the state, so of the way the parameter $h_x$ is encoded.


\subsection{Truncated Quantum Fisher Information (TQFI)}
For mixed states, rank truncation can provide an efficient lower and upper bounds on the fidelity.

\begin{definition}[Rank-Truncated States]
    Let $\rho_\theta = \sum_{k=1}^r \lambda_k |\lambda_k\rangle\langle\lambda_k|$ be ordered such that $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_r > 0$. For a truncation rank $m \le r$, the subnormalized projected state is defined as:
    \begin{equation}
        \rho_\theta^{(m)} = \sum_{k=1}^m \lambda_k |\lambda_k\rangle\langle\lambda_k| = \Pi^{(m)} \rho_\theta \Pi^{(m)}
    \end{equation}
    where $\Pi^{(m)} = \sum_{k=1}^m |\lambda_k\rangle\langle\lambda_k|$ is the dominant eigenspace projector.
    The shifted state is projected with the same projector constructed from $\rho_\theta$:
    \begin{equation}
        \rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)}
        =\Pi^{(m)}\rho_{\theta+\delta}\Pi^{(m)}
        \label{eq:shifted_rank_truncated_state}
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}
\begin{remark}[Analogy to PCA]
    This construction is analogous to PCA or a truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), it retains the eigenspace carrying the largest spectral weight. Because a density matrix is Hermitian and positive semidefinite, its singular values equal its eigenvalues. The analogy refers to spectral compression, because the dominant eigenspace spanned by its most important eigenvectors, do not necessarily contain all  sensitivity to the parameter.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Truncated Fidelity Bounds]
    The exact fidelity is bounded from below by the fidelity of the truncated states and from above by the generalized fidelity $F_*$, which adds a correction for their missing trace weight~\cite{beckey2022vqfiea}:
    \begin{equation}
        F\left(\rho_\theta^{(m)}, \rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)}\right) \le F(\rho_\theta, \rho_{\theta+\delta}) \le F_*\left(\rho_\theta^{(m)}, \rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)}\right)
        \label{eq:truncated_fidelity_inequality}
    \end{equation}
    where the generalized fidelity for subnormalized positive operators $\sigma$ and $\tau$ is defined as
    \begin{equation}
        F_*(\sigma, \tau) \coloneqq \operatorname{Tr}\left[\sqrt{\sqrt{\sigma}\tau\sqrt{\sigma}}\right] + \sqrt{(1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\sigma])(1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\tau])}
    \end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[TQFI Bounds]
    Applying the finite-displacement functional to the fidelity bounds defines the Truncated Quantum Fisher Information (TQFI) lower and upper bounds:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F_*; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right)
        \label{eq:tqfi_bounds}
    \end{equation}
    where the induced difference quotient for a fidelity functional $f$ is
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{I}_\delta(f;\rho_\theta)
        \coloneqq \frac{8\left[1-f(\rho_\theta,\rho_{\theta+\delta})\right]}{\delta^2}
        \label{eq:qfi_difference_quotient}
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Sub- and Super-Fidelity Bounds (SSQFI)}
To avoid eigenspace truncation entirely, we implement bounds based on polynomial trace functionals of the density matrices.

\begin{definition}[Sub- and Super-Fidelity]
    For any two density operators $\rho$ and $\sigma$, the Uhlmann fidelity is bounded by the subfidelity $E(\rho,\sigma)$ and superfidelity $R(\rho,\sigma)$:
    \begin{equation}
        \sqrt{E(\rho,\sigma)} \le F(\rho,\sigma) \le \sqrt{R(\rho,\sigma)}
    \end{equation}
    where the explicit trace algebra expressions are:
    \begin{align}
        E(\rho,\sigma) & = \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma] + \sqrt{2 \left( \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma] \right)^2 - 2 \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma\rho\sigma]} \\
        R(\rho,\sigma) & = \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma] + \sqrt{\left( 1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\rho^2] \right)\left( 1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\sigma^2] \right)}
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[SSQFI Bounds and Unified Interval]
    At finite displacement, the sub- and super-fidelities induce the SSQFI bounds
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{R}; \rho_\theta\right) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{E}; \rho_\theta\right)
    \end{equation}
    Combining TQFI and SSQFI gives the tightest interval supplied by these two constructions:
    \begin{align}
        H_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) & = \max \left\{ \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F_*; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right), \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{R}; \rho_\theta\right) \right\}
        \label{eq:vqfie_combined_lower}                                                                                                                                     \\
        J_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) & = \min \left\{ \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right), \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{E}; \rho_\theta\right) \right\}
        \label{eq:vqfie_combined_upper}
    \end{align}
    So we have $H_\delta\leq\mathcal{I}_\delta(\theta;\rho_\theta)\leq J_\delta$. The corresponding bounds on the local QFI are obtained in the limit $\delta\to0$. At nonzero $\delta$, $[H_\delta,J_\delta]$ bounds the finite-displacement functional rather than the exact SLD QFI.
\end{definition}

\section{The VQFIE Estimation and Optimization Loop}

VQFIE can be used only to estimate the interval $[H_\delta,J_\delta]$ for a fixed probe. Optionally the lower endpoint $H_\delta(\theta;\rho_\theta)$ can be embedded in a closed-loop variational pipeline that prepares probe states $\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=U(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rho_{\mathrm{in}}U^\dagger(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$. At each outer-loop iteration, VQSE supplies the dominant eigenspace of the encoded state, non-variational circuits evaluate the required overlaps, and a classical optimizer updates the preparation parameters using the lower bound as its objective (see Fig.~\ref{fig:vqfie_schema}). This can be useful because it is a cheap way to improve the probe state for parameter estimation, having a minimal resource overhead (but requires copies), even since it is a lower bound.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{figures/asi_report/vqfie.png}
    \caption{Schematic representation of the VQFIE estimation and optional probe-optimization loop, figure from~\cite{beckey2022vqfiea}.}
    \label{fig:vqfie_schema}
\end{figure}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Variational Quantum Fisher Information Estimation (VQFIE)}
    \label{alg:vqfie}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Initial probe $\rho_{\text{in}}$, optional preparation ansatz $U(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$, field shift $\delta$, truncation rank $m$.
        \For{iteration $k = 1, 2, \dots, N_{\text{max}}$}
        \State If optimization is enabled, prepare $\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=U(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\rho_{\text{in}}U^\dagger(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$; otherwise set $\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\rho_{\text{in}}$.
        \State Apply physical encoding channels to generate $\rho_\theta = \mathcal{E}_\theta(\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})$ and $\rho_{\theta+\delta} = \mathcal{E}_{\theta+\delta}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})$.
        \State Run \textbf{VQSE} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:vqse}) on $\rho_\theta$ to extract $\{\tilde{\lambda}_i\}_{i=1}^m$ and preparation circuits for $\{|\tilde{\lambda}_i\rangle\}_{i=1}^m$.
        \State For all $i,j \in \{1,\dots,m\}$, execute overlap circuits to estimate
        \[
            M_{ij}=\langle\tilde{\lambda}_i|\rho_{\theta+\delta}|\tilde{\lambda}_j\rangle,
            \qquad
            T_{ij}=\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_i\tilde{\lambda}_j}\,M_{ij}.
        \]
        \State Classically compute the truncated fidelity $F=\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{T}$ and
        \[
            F_*=F+\sqrt{\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^m\tilde{\lambda}_i\right)
                \left(1-\sum_{i=1}^m M_{ii}\right)}.
        \]
        \State Evaluate the TQFI bounds and, when the unified interval is required, the SSQFI bounds; combine them according to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:vqfie_combined_lower} and~\eqref{eq:vqfie_combined_upper}.
        \If{probe optimization is enabled}
        \State Use $H_\delta$ as the objective when both bound families are evaluated; otherwise use the TQFI lower endpoint $8(1-F_*)/\delta^2$.
        \State Update $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ with the selected classical optimizer.
        \EndIf
        \EndFor
        \State \textbf{Return:} The evaluated interval and, when optimized, $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\text{opt}}$.
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Quantum Circuit Implementation}
% ==============================================================================

The variational circuit used to execute VQFIE is implemented using a Layered Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (LHEA).

\begin{definition}[LHEA Circuit Architecture]
    An $L$-layer LHEA acting on $n$ qubits is expressed as:
    \begin{equation}
        U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \prod_{\ell=1}^L \left( U_{\text{ent}} \prod_{j=1}^n R_y(\alpha_{\ell, j}^{(y)}) R_z(\alpha_{\ell, j}^{(z)}) \right)
    \end{equation}
    where $R_y$ and $R_z$ are single-qubit rotations and $U_{\text{ent}}=\prod_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{c}}}\operatorname{CNOT}_{i,j}$ entangles qubits connected by the circuit-edge set $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{c}}$. For the implementation considered here, $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{c}}$ contains nearest-neighbor pairs along a 1D chain; see Fig.~\ref{fig:lhea_circuit}.
    \label{def:lhea}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Quantum Overlap Subroutines (SWAP and Generalized SWAP Tests)}
The evaluation of the SSQFI bounds and the off-diagonal $T$ matrix elements $T_{ij}$ requires measuring linear and nonlinear state overlap functionals.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Destructive SWAP Test ($\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^2]$ and $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma]$):}
          To evaluate purity $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta^2]$ and pairwise overlaps $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta}]$, we apply a Bell-basis measurement across corresponding qubits of two registers containing the required states. The advantage is that it avoids an ancillary control qubit, but it is destructive. The overlap is extracted from the parity of the observed bitstrings:
          \begin{equation}
              \operatorname{Tr}[\rho \sigma] = \sum_{x, y \in \{0,1\}^n} (-1)^{x \cdot y} P_{\text{Bell}}(x, y)
          \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Generalized Controlled-SWAP Circuit ($\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma\rho\sigma]$):}
          To compute the fourth-order overlap $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta} \rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta}]$ required for the subfidelity $E(\rho,\sigma)$, we employ four state registers controlled by one ancilla qubit prepared in $|+\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)$. Applying a controlled cyclic permutation $S_4|i,j,k,l\rangle=|l,i,j,k\rangle$ and measuring the ancilla in the $X$ basis yields
          \begin{equation}
              P(\text{Ancilla} = 0) = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta} \rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta}] \right).
          \end{equation}

          This fourth-order term is part of the subfidelity and therefore cannot be omitted when computing the SSQFI upper endpoint. Because it requires four state registers, the TQFI construction can be more economical when a sufficiently small truncation rank gives tight bounds.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodological Scope}
The exact SLD calculation is the reference method for the finite systems studied in Chapter~\ref{ch:noise_analysis}. The finite-displacement TQFI and SSQFI quantities test how much of the fidelity response can be recovered without full diagonalization, while VQSE is confronted to numerical diagonalization, for supplying the dominant spectral data needed by TQFI. The outer VQFIE state-preparation loop is optional: estimating the bounds for a fixed preparation does not require optimizing $U(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$. 

We built, optimized and tested all the necessary tools, quantum circuit and the corresponding classical mathematical simulation frameworks. In this way we can swap between them at any stage of the analysis, allowing for flexible testing and validation of the methods under different conditions.
