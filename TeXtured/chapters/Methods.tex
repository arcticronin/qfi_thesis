\chapter{Methods: Variational Estimation of Mixed-State QFI} \label{ch:methods}


This chapter develops the computational methodology used to estimate the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) for mixed states. Exact QFI provides a useful benchmark, but its evaluation requires operations on the full density matrix and becomes computationally costly as the system size increases. So, we consider fidelity-based bounds that can be evaluated from the dominant components or trace functionals of the state.

We define as a toy model a finite one-dimensional Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM), that can be considered as our controlled magnetometer. Reduced mixed states are generated by tracing out part of an extended probe, and their exact QFI is computed just as a numerical reference. This setting makes it possible to compare exact calculations with the Truncated Quantum Fisher Information (TQFI) and sub- and super-fidelity QFI (SSQFI) bounds.

The chapter then develops the variational implementation of these bounds. We implement the Variational Quantum State Eigensolver (VQSE) to identify the dominant eigenspace required by TQFI and place it within the Variational Quantum Fisher Information Estimation (VQFIE) pipeline~\cite{beckey2022vqfiea,cerezo2022vqse}. The resulting quantum--classical procedure estimates and optimizes mixed-state metrological bounds without full density-matrix diagonalization. The TFIM construction, exact benchmark, variational algorithms, and required circuit primitives presented here provide the methodological basis for the numerical analysis in Chapter~\ref{ch:noise_analysis}.

\section{Physical Toy Model: Open 1-D TFIM}
% ==============================================================================

We model the quantum sensor as a 1D $N$-qubit Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM) with open boundary conditions. The external parameter to be estimated, $\theta$, corresponds to the transverse magnetic field strength $h$ acting uniformly across the chain. After the evolution, only an $n$-qubit subsystem, with $n < N$, is retained as the accessible probe.

\begin{definition}[Transverse-Field Ising Hamiltonian]
    The Hamiltonian governing the total $N$-qubit system is defined as:
    \begin{equation}
        H(h) = J \sum_{i=0}^{N-2} X_i X_{i+1} + h \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} Z_i,
        \label{eq:tfim_hamiltonian}
    \end{equation}
    where $J$ denotes the nearest-neighbor exchange coupling strength, and $X_i, Z_i$ represent Pauli operators acting on the $i$-th site.
\end{definition}

\subsection{State Initialization and Mixed-State Generation via Partial Trace}
To simulate an effective open-system probe, we initialize the total $N$-qubit system in the reference product state $|0\rangle^{\otimes N}$ and allow it to evolve under the unitary dynamics generated by $H(h)$.


We keep the $n$-qubit subsystem $S=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ and trace out the complementary subsystem $E=\{n,\ldots,N-1\}$, discarding $N-n$ qubits.

\begin{definition}[Reduced Probe State Generation]
    The exact parameter-dependent mixed state $\rho_\theta$ acting on the retained $n$-qubit sensor subspace $\mathcal{H}_S$ is generated by tracing out all $N-n$ qubits in $E$ from the evolved pure state:
    \begin{equation}
        \rho_\theta = \operatorname{Tr}_{E} \left[ U_\theta \left( |0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes N} \right) U_\theta^\dagger \right],
        \qquad E=\{n,\ldots,N-1\}
        \label{eq:partial_trace_state}
    \end{equation}
    Where $U_\theta = e^{-iH(h)t}$ is the time-evolution operator for time $t$.
    Similarly, the shifted state $\rho_{\theta+\delta}$ is generated under the perturbed magnetic field $h + \delta$:
    \begin{equation}
        \rho_{\theta+\delta} = \operatorname{Tr}_{E} \left[ U_{\theta+\delta} \left( |0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes N} \right) U_{\theta+\delta}^\dagger \right]
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Participation Ratio and Effective Rank]
    The partial trace integrates out the $N-n$ inaccessible degrees of freedom and generally transforms the total pure state into a mixed state on the retained subsystem. The degree of mixing is quantified by the participation ratio $R = 1 / \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_\theta^2)$, which indicates the effective number of dominant eigenvalues required for accurate rank truncation. Trivially if it is pure we only need one eigenvalue since $\rho = \ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact Baseline: Ground-Truth QFI Evaluation}
% ==============================================================================

To establish a numerical ground truth against which variational approximations can be benchmarked, we compute the exact QFI of the reduced state $\rho_\theta$. The SLD definition and spectral expression are given in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:sld_lyapunov} and \eqref{eq:qfi_spectral_explicit}.
For the small reference systems we also use the vectorized QFIM formula in Eq.~\eqref{eq:safranek_vectorized}.

\subsection{Numerical Differentiation and Exact Benchmarking}
When explicit analytical derivatives of the reduced density matrix $\rho_\theta$ are intractable, we approximate the parameter derivative $\partial_\theta \rho_\theta$ in our numerical pipeline using a second-order central difference scheme:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \rho_\theta}{\partial \theta} \approx \frac{\rho_{\theta+\delta} - \rho_{\theta-\delta}}{2\delta} + \mathcal{O}(\delta^2).
    \label{eq:central_difference}
\end{equation}

Evaluating the exact SLD matrix elements and QFI requires full classical diagonalization of the $2^n \times 2^n$ density matrix, scaling as $\mathcal{O}(8^n)$. This classical bottleneck motivates the variational bounds implemented in this chapter.

\section{Dynamics-Agnostic Bounds: TQFI and SSQFI} \label{sec:bounds}
% ==============================================================================

On near-term hardware, exact diagonalization is intractable. To overcome this bottleneck, we implement dynamics-agnostic lower and upper bounds derived from the Bures fidelity $F(\rho_\theta, \rho_{\theta+\delta})$. Its connection to QFI is descibed in Eq.~\eqref{def:bures_distance} and \eqref{def:qfim_bures_relation}, and are described in~\cite{beckey2022vqfiea}. We denote the finite-difference QFI functional by $\mathcal{I}_\delta(\theta;\rho_\theta)$, using $\mathcal{F}_Q(\theta)$ just in its infinitesimal limit.


\subsection{Truncated Quantum Fisher Information (TQFI)}
For mixed states, rank truncation can provide an efficient lower and upper bounds on the fidelity.

\begin{definition}[Rank-Truncated States]
    Let $\rho_\theta = \sum_{k=1}^r \lambda_k |\lambda_k\rangle\langle\lambda_k|$ be ordered such that $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_r > 0$. For a truncation rank $m \le r$, the subnormalized projected state is defined as:
    \begin{equation}
        \rho_\theta^{(m)} = \sum_{k=1}^m \lambda_k |\lambda_k\rangle\langle\lambda_k| = \Pi^{(m)} \rho_\theta \Pi^{(m)},
    \end{equation}
    where $\Pi^{(m)} = \sum_{k=1}^m |\lambda_k\rangle\langle\lambda_k|$ is the dominant eigenspace projector.
\end{definition}
\begin{remark}[Analogy to PCA]
    The construction of the rank-truncated state $\rho_\theta^{(m)}$ is mathematically equivalent to dimensionality reduction techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA) or truncated Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). Because the density matrix is Hermitian and positive semi-definite, its eigendecomposition is identical to its SVD. Applying the dominant eigenspace projector $\Pi^{(m)}$ isolates the $m$ principal components associated with the largest eigenvalues (the highest classical probabilities). Just as PCA approximates a dataset using only the directions of maximum variance to filter out noise, this truncation captures the most significant metrological features of $\rho_\theta$ while discarding the low-probability eigenspace.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Truncated Fidelity Bounds]
    The exact Uhlmann fidelity is bounded from below by the standard fidelity of the truncated states, and from above by the generalized subnormalized fidelity $F_*$, where it is added a correction term (\cite{beckey2022vqfiea}):
    \begin{equation}
        F\left(\rho_\theta^{(m)}, \rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)}\right) \le F(\rho_\theta, \rho_{\theta+\delta}) \le F_*\left(\rho_\theta^{(m)}, \rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)}\right),
        \label{eq:truncated_fidelity_inequality}
    \end{equation}
    where the generalized fidelity for a pair of density matrices $\sigma, \tau$ is defined as:
    \begin{equation}
        F_*(\sigma, \tau) \coloneqq \operatorname{Tr}\left[\sqrt{\sqrt{\sigma}\tau\sqrt{\sigma}}\right] + \sqrt{(1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\sigma])(1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\tau])}
    \end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[TQFI Bounds]
    Expanding the fidelity bounds to second order in $\delta$ defines the Truncated Quantum Fisher Information (TQFI) lower and upper bounds:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F_*; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right).
        \label{eq:tqfi_bounds}
    \end{equation}
    where the induced difference quotient for a fidelity functional $f$ is
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{I}_\delta(f;\rho_\theta)
        \coloneqq \frac{8\left[1-f(\rho_\theta,\rho_{\theta+\delta})\right]}{\delta^2}.
        \label{eq:qfi_difference_quotient}
    \end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Sub- and Super-Fidelity Bounds (SSQFI)}
To avoid eigenspace truncation entirely, we implement bounds based on polynomial trace functionals of the density matrices.

\begin{definition}[Sub- and Super-Fidelity]
    For any two density operators $\rho$ and $\sigma$, the Uhlmann fidelity is bounded by the subfidelity $E(\rho,\sigma)$ and superfidelity $R(\rho,\sigma)$:
    \begin{equation}
        \sqrt{E(\rho,\sigma)} \le F(\rho,\sigma) \le \sqrt{R(\rho,\sigma)},
    \end{equation}
    where the explicit trace algebra expressions are:
    \begin{align}
        E(\rho,\sigma) & = \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma] + \sqrt{2 \left( \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma] \right)^2 - 2 \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma\rho\sigma]}, \\
        R(\rho,\sigma) & = \operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma] + \sqrt{\left( 1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\rho^2] \right)\left( 1 - \operatorname{Tr}[\sigma^2] \right)}.
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[SSQFI Bounds and Unified Interval]
    In the asymptotic limit $\delta \to 0$, the sub- and super-fidelities induce the SSQFI bounds:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{R}; \rho_\theta\right) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) \le \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{E}; \rho_\theta\right).
    \end{equation}
    Combining TQFI and SSQFI yields an optimal, dynamics-agnostic uncertainty interval $[H_\delta, J_\delta]$ for the true QFI:
    \begin{align}
        H_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) & = \max \left\{ \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F_*; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right), \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{R}; \rho_\theta\right) \right\}, \\
        J_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta) & = \min \left\{ \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(F; \rho_\theta^{(m)}\right), \mathcal{I}_\delta\left(\sqrt{E}; \rho_\theta\right) \right\}.
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\section{The VQFIE Metrological Optimization Loop}

We embed the TQFI lower bound $H_\delta(\theta; \rho_\theta)$ into a closed-loop variational pipeline to prepare probe states $\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) \rho_{\text{in}} U^\dagger(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$ that maximize magnetic-field sensitivity. At each outer-loop iteration, VQSE supplies the dominant eigenspace of the encoded state, overlap circuits evaluate the truncated fidelity, and the classical optimizer updates the preparation parameters using the resulting lower bound, see Figure~\ref{fig:vqfie_schema}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{figures/asi_report/vqfie.png}
    \caption{Schematic representation of the VQFIE loop, from the paper.}
    \label{fig:vqfie_schema}
\end{figure}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
    \caption{Variational Quantum Fisher Information Estimation (VQFIE)}
    \label{alg:vqfie}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require Initial probe $\rho_{\text{in}}$, preparation ansatz $U(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$, field shift $\delta$, truncation rank $m$.
        \For{iteration $t = 1, 2, \dots, N_{\text{max}}$}
        \State Prepare state $\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) \rho_{\text{in}} U^\dagger(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$.
        \State Apply physical encoding channels to generate $\rho_\theta = \mathcal{E}_\theta(\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})$ and $\rho_{\theta+\delta} = \mathcal{E}_{\theta+\delta}(\rho_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})$.
        \State Run \textbf{VQSE} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:vqse}) on $\rho_\theta$ to extract $\{\tilde{\lambda}_i\}_{i=1}^m$ and preparation circuits for $\{|\tilde{\lambda}_i\rangle\}_{i=1}^m$.
        \State For all $i,j \in \{1,\dots,m\}$, execute overlap circuits to measure matrix elements:
        $$T_{ij} = \sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_i \tilde{\lambda}_j} \langle \tilde{\lambda}_i | \rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)} | \tilde{\lambda}_j \rangle.$$
        \State Classically compute truncated Uhlmann fidelity $F(\rho_\theta^{(m)}, \rho_{\theta+\delta}^{(m)}) = \operatorname{Tr}[\sqrt{T^\dagger T}]$ and $F_*$.
        \State Evaluate TQFI lower bound $H_\delta(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) = 8[1 - F_*(\boldsymbol{\alpha})] / \delta^2$.
        \State Update $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ to maximize $H_\delta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$ with the selected classical optimizer.
        \EndFor
        \State \textbf{Return:} Optimal sensing-state preparation parameters $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\text{opt}}$ and QFI interval $[H_\delta, J_\delta]$.
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Hardware-Efficient Circuit Implementations}
% ==============================================================================

To execute VQFIE on NISQ hardware, we specify the circuit architectures for state preparation, VQSE diagonalization, and non-variational overlap tests.

\subsection{Layered Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (LHEA)}
Both the probe preparation unitary $U(\boldsymbol{\alpha})$ and the VQSE diagonalizing unitary $V(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ are implemented using a Layered Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (LHEA).

\begin{definition}[LHEA Circuit Architecture]
    An $L$-layer LHEA acting on $n$ qubits is expressed as:
    \begin{equation}
        U(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \prod_{\ell=1}^L \left( U_{\text{ent}} \prod_{j=1}^n R_y(\alpha_{\ell, j}^{(y)}) R_z(\alpha_{\ell, j}^{(z)}) \right),
    \end{equation}
    where $R_y, R_z$ are single-qubit rotations and $U_{\text{ent}} = \prod_{(i,j) \in E} \text{CNOT}_{i,j}$ entangles nearest-neighbor qubits along a 1D chain topology (see Fig.~\ref{fig:lhea_circuit}).
    \label{def:lhea}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Quantum Overlap Subroutines (SWAP and Generalized SWAP Tests)}
The evaluation of the SSQFI bounds and the off-diagonal $T$ matrix elements $T_{ij}$ requires measuring linear and nonlinear state overlap functionals.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Destructive SWAP Test ($\operatorname{Tr}[\rho^2]$ and $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma]$):}
          To evaluate purity $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta^2]$ and pairwise overlaps $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta}]$, we apply a Bell-basis measurement across corresponding qubits of two identical registers without requiring an ancilla qubit. The overlap is extracted from the parity of the observed bitstrings:
          \begin{equation}
              \operatorname{Tr}[\rho \sigma] = \sum_{x, y \in \{0,1\}^n} (-1)^{x \cdot y} P_{\text{Bell}}(x, y).
          \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Generalized Controlled-SWAP Circuit ($\operatorname{Tr}[\rho\sigma\rho\sigma]$):}
          To compute the fourth-order overlap $\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta} \rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta}]$ required for the subfidelity $E(\rho,\sigma)$, we employ a 4-copy register controlled by a single ancilla qubit prepared in $|+\rangle = (|0\rangle + |1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}$. Applying a controlled cyclic permutation shift operator $S_4 |i,j,k,l\rangle = |l,i,j,k\rangle$ and measuring the ancilla in the X-basis yields:
          \begin{equation}
              P(\text{Ancilla} = 0) = \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta} \rho_\theta \rho_{\theta+\delta}] \right).
          \end{equation}

          For this reason we will focus more on the Truncated Subfidelity $E(\rho,\sigma)$ rather than the full fourth-order overlap, as it provides a more practical and resource-efficient approach for near-term quantum devices.
\end{enumerate}
