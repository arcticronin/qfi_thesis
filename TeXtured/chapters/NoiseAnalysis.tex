\chapter{Local Quantum Metrology under Reduction, Temperature, and Noise}
\label{ch:noise_analysis}

This chapter asks how much information about the transverse field $h_x$ is available to an observer who can access only an $n$-qubit subsystem of an $N$-qubit TFIM probe. The central issue is therefore local metrology: not only how much QFI is present in the full state, but how much survives after part of the probe is discarded and how that answer depends on the physical origin of mixedness.

The numerical framework follows Chapter~\ref{ch:methods}. Exact SLD QFI is the primary differential benchmark, evaluated using the formalism of Chapter~\ref{ch:MathBackground}; the finite-displacement fidelity quantities follow Section~\ref{sec:bounds}. This chapter specifies the preparation histories and the resulting comparisons, because the order of preparation, channel action, and partial trace determines what can be concluded.

\begin{remark}[What this chapter establishes]
    For the finite TFIM geometries, field windows, and state families sampled here, local SLD QFI grows strongly when more qubits are accessible. Depolarizing noise is a monotonic damaging baseline. Thermal preparation of the \emph{full} system before partial trace can outperform the reduced ground-state family in selected regimes. These are finite-system, state-family-dependent results; they are not universal scaling laws or a claim that thermal noise is generically beneficial.
\end{remark}

\section{Question, State Families, and Valid Comparisons}
\label{sec:noise_state_families}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Subsystem-first metrology}

Let $S$ denote the retained $n$-qubit subsystem and $E$ the discarded complement of $N-n$ qubits. The parameter $h_x$ plays the role of $\theta$ in Chapters~\ref{ch:MathBackground} and~\ref{ch:methods}; the notation $h_x$ is kept here to match the numerical data sets.

\begin{definition}[Prepared and reduced state families]
    The ground-state reference, depolarized, and thermal families are
    \begin{align}
        \rho_{\mathrm{G},N}(h_x)
         & \coloneqq |\psi_0(h_x)\rangle\langle\psi_0(h_x)|,               &
        \rho_{\mathrm{sub}}(h_x)
         & \coloneqq \operatorname{Tr}_E[\rho_{\mathrm{G},N}(h_x)],          \\
        \rho_{\mathrm{dep},N}(h_x;P)
         & \coloneqq \Lambda^{(N)}_P[\rho_{\mathrm{G},N}(h_x)],            &
        \rho_{\mathrm{dep},S}(h_x;P)
         & \coloneqq \operatorname{Tr}_E[\rho_{\mathrm{dep},N}(h_x;P)],      \\
        \rho_{\mathrm{th},N}(h_x;\beta)
         & \coloneqq \frac{\exp[-\beta H_N(h_x)]}{Z(h_x,\beta)},           &
        Z(h_x,\beta)
         & \coloneqq \operatorname{Tr}[\exp[-\beta H_N(h_x)]],               \\
        \rho_{\mathrm{th},S}(h_x;\beta)
         & \coloneqq \operatorname{Tr}_E[\rho_{\mathrm{th},N}(h_x;\beta)].
        \label{eq:noise_state_families}
    \end{align}
    Here $\Lambda^{(N)}_P$ is a full-system depolarizing channel with fixed probability $P$, and $\beta$ is the inverse temperature of the Gibbs preparation.
\end{definition}

The three primary local comparators are $\rho_{\mathrm{sub}}$, $\rho_{\mathrm{dep},S}$, and $\rho_{\mathrm{th},S}$. Full-system curves are reference ceilings and consistency checks; they are not the primary comparator for a local quantity.

\subsection{Data processing and the comparison rule}

For a fixed $P$, the depolarizing channel and the partial trace are parameter-independent channels. The valid full-to-local comparison is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{dep},S}(h_x;P)]
    \leq \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{dep},N}(h_x;P)].
    \label{eq:depolarizing_data_processing}
\end{equation}
Likewise,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{sub}}(h_x)]
    \leq \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{G},N}(h_x)],
    \qquad
    \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{th},S}(h_x;\beta)]
    \leq \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{th},N}(h_x;\beta)].
    \label{eq:local_global_data_processing}
\end{equation}
All latest Project~9 outputs satisfy these checks at every sampled field.

\begin{remark}[A necessary distinction]
    Thermal preparation is not generally a parameter-independent channel applied to the ground-state family: the Hamiltonian and Gibbs state both depend on $h_x$. Consequently, there is no theorem requiring
    \[
        \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{th},S}(h_x;\beta)]
        \leq \mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{sub}}(h_x)].
    \]
    If the thermal local QFI exceeds the reduced-ground-state local QFI, the two sides are distinct encoded families. This does not violate Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_global_data_processing}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Fixed-noise and matched-purity modes}

The fixed-noise mode holds $P$ or $\beta$ constant throughout the $h_x$ sweep and is the primary physical experiment. Project~9 also contains a matched-purity control:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{dep},S}^2(h_x;P(h_x))]
    =\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{sub}}^2(h_x)],
    \qquad
    \operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{th},S}^2(h_x;\beta(h_x))]
    =\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{\mathrm{sub}}^2(h_x)].
    \label{eq:purity_matching_cond}
\end{equation}
This asks whether equal local purity is sufficient to imply equal local QFI. It is a structural control, not a fixed physical-channel experiment, because $P$ and $\beta$ are selected as functions of $h_x$.

\begin{remark}[Derivative along the matched path]
    The SLD must follow the parameterized path actually being compared. For the matched depolarizing family,
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mathrm{dep},S}}{\mathrm{d}h_x}
        =(1-P)\frac{\partial\rho_{\mathrm{sub}}}{\partial h_x}
        -\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}h_x}\rho_{\mathrm{sub}}
        +\frac{1}{2^n}\frac{\mathrm{d}P}{\mathrm{d}h_x}\mathbb{I}_S,
        \label{eq:matched_depolarizing_derivative}
    \end{equation}
    and the thermal derivative includes
    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mathrm{th},S}}{\mathrm{d}h_x}
        =\operatorname{Tr}_E\!\left[
            \frac{\partial\rho_{\mathrm{th},N}}{\partial h_x}
            +\frac{\partial\rho_{\mathrm{th},N}}{\partial\beta}
            \frac{\mathrm{d}\beta}{\mathrm{d}h_x}\right].
        \label{eq:matched_thermal_derivative}
    \end{equation}
    Omitting these chain-rule terms would calculate the QFI of a different family. The current Project~9 implementation includes them, and the full-to-local checks provide a practical validation of the corrected derivatives.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[TQFI requires calibrated language]
    At the selected finite displacements, \texttt{reduced\_lower\_tqfi} can exceed exact local SLD QFI. The largest observed excesses are $0.112$ in the latest Project~1 size sweep, $1.068$ in the Project~1 subsystem-size sweep, and $1.071$ in the open-boundary Project~7 data. In contrast, \texttt{reduced\_sub\_qfi\_bound} never exceeds local SLD QFI in the current arrays. Exact SLD QFI therefore supports the physical claims; TQFI is a finite-displacement, truncation-aware proxy whose tightness is itself a result.
\end{remark}

The data set consists of saved \texttt{.npz} outputs generated between 2026-08-22 and 2026-08-25: \texttt{project1\_scaling\_N\_20260822\_162213.npz} and \texttt{project1\_scaling\_n\_20260822\_162301.npz}; six Project~7 outputs dated 2026-08-23; and twenty-one Project~9 outputs dated 2026-08-25. Projects~1 and~7 use $\delta h_x=0.1$ and $m=2$. Project~9 uses $\delta h_x=0.05$ and normally $m=3$. Exact SLD values may be compared when the physical families agree, but raw TQFI magnitudes should not be pooled across projects without accounting for the different displacement and truncation settings.

The results demonstrate a finite-temperature spectral-susceptibility effect, not that the Gibbs state is more informative than the ground state at every fixed field as $\beta\rightarrow\infty$.
\end{remark}

    \max_{h_x}\mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{th},S}]
    =4.847
    <
    8.456
    =\max_{h_x}\mathcal{F}_Q[\rho_{\mathrm{th},N}].

The finite-system mechanism is the field dependence of low-lying thermal populations, not an increase of QFI under a fixed channel.

\begin{remark}[Precise thermal-benefit claim]
    For the finite TFIM geometries and field windows studied here, thermal preparation before partial trace can enhance local SLD QFI relative to the entanglement-induced reduced ground-state subsystem. The effect is regime dependent, is strongest at larger $\beta$ in the present sweep, and remains bounded by the QFI of the full thermal family.
\end{remark}

\section{Synthesis, Limitations, and Research Takeaways}
\label{sec:noise_synthesis}
% ==============================================================================

\begin{remark}[Supported conclusion: accessible degrees of freedom]
    Projects~1 and~9 show that critical metrological response is spatially distributed. Partial trace removes a variable fraction of it, and the retained fraction grows strongly with accessible subsystem size. Keeping all but one qubit recovers essentially all of the full-system peak QFI in the tested $N=8$ open-chain and $N=6$ periodic cases.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Supported conclusion: the origin of mixedness matters]
    Entanglement-induced mixedness, depolarization, and thermal population are not interchangeable. Depolarization is Hamiltonian blind and monotonically damaging in the sampled sweep. Thermal preparation changes how low-lying levels encode $h_x$ and can be favorable in selected field and boundary-condition regimes. Purity alone does not predict local QFI.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Supported conclusion: boundary conditions are a spectral-control resource]
    Boundary conditions change finite-size gaps, low-energy sectors, peak positions, and thermal recovery scales. In the present systems, they can reverse the ordering between reduced ground-state and reduced thermal families.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Claims that need qualification]
    The present data do not support a universal QFI scaling exponent. Thermal preparation is not generically beneficial and should never be described as a fixed channel increasing QFI of the same encoded family. Local TQFI should not be compared directly with global SLD QFI, and \texttt{lower\_tqfi} should not be called a strict pointwise SLD lower bound at the present finite displacements. Finally, a peak at the edge of the field scan is a reason to validate the scan window, not a final peak location.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Methodological takeaway]
    Exact local SLD QFI is the correct benchmark where it can be computed. Finite-displacement TQFI adds a complementary operational layer by testing whether a truncated or fidelity-based description retains the same feature. Such quantities should be validated against exact local SLD on small systems before they are used where exact SLD is unavailable.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Next calculations}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Extend Project~9 Preset~E to $\beta=10,25,50$ and study pointwise convergence to the reduced ground-state family at fixed $h_x$.
    \item Repeat the size sweep at fixed $n/N$ and, separately, fixed $n$, including at least one larger feasible size with the memory-aware worker cap, before fitting peak-height or peak-position scaling.
    \item Save both the present operational $\beta_\star$ and a crossover based on exact local SLD or \texttt{sub\_qfi\_bound}.
    \item Extend field windows for boundary-condition runs whose thermal maximum is at $h_x=0.1$, or justify the lower boundary analytically.
    \item Add a compact spectrum panel showing the first excitation gap beside the strongest thermal-enhancement curves.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Practical design principle]
    For finite quantum sensors, local metrological performance is determined by the full sequence that produces the accessible state. Preserve access to the relevant subsystem, engineer the low-energy spectrum, and distinguish structured thermal preparation from structureless external mixing. The origin of mixedness is itself a metrological resource variable.
\end{remark}
