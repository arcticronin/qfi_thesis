%% Abstract (recommended length around 80-200 words; this is not a copy of your thesis assignment!)
\newcommand*{\Abstract}{%
    Quantum Fisher information (QFI) bounds quantum-sensor sensitivity.  This thesis develops scalable mixed-state QFI methods and studies how preparation, access, noise, and measurement affect sensitivity.  It implements the Variational Quantum State Eigensolver in a variational pipeline for truncated and fidelity-based QFI bounds.

    A finite Transverse-Field Ising Model serves as a magnetometer.  We compare the reduced ground state with full-probe depolarization and Gibbs preparation of the full probe before inaccessible qubits are traced out.  For periodic $N=6$, $n=4$ over $h_x/J\in[0.1,2.5]$, $P=0.3$ lowers the peak local QFI by $36.4\%$ relative to $P=0$.  At $\beta=5$, the Gibbs-prepared state has more local QFI at $40$ of $60$ sampled fields and a $23.5\%$ higher peak.  The best preparation therefore depends on the operating range.

    The optical study estimates exoplanet separation with spatial-mode demultiplexing (SPADE).  Archived ASI Matera aggregate first-order counts cannot support experimental TQFI or channel reconstruction.  We instead fit a classical calibration model and organize a quantum-to-classical channel sequence.  Grouped holdout coverage is about $94\%$ for nominal $95\%$ intervals.  In the assumed source model, full ideal SPADE retains at least $99.899\%$ of the source QFI per surviving photon.  Binary CFI after assumed readout cross-talk retains only $0.13\%$--$57.0\%$.  A future VQ-SPADE experiment is proposed.
}
